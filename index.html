The top line

Open tanker war has throttled Hormuz to about ten ships a day, with Brent above $98, as Germany's AfD wins Saxony-Anhalt with the highest far-right vote share since 1945.

The World

1 · US-Iran tanker war throttles Hormuz; Brent pushes above $98

US forces disabled or destroyed three Iranian-linked tankers at the weekend, after Iran fired ballistic missiles at two US warships, which CENTCOM says evaded unharmed while the Revolutionary Guard claims hits. Iran's security council secretary announced a coming Hormuz exclusion zone. Kpler's ten-day transit average fell to 10 vessels a day on 6 September, the lowest since May. Brent settled $97.31 Monday, then traded above $98 Tuesday. Iran says an Oman safe-passage accord is near.

Reuters via gCaptain (center, T1) · Al Jazeera (center-left, T1) · CBS News (center, T1) · PBS/AP (center, T1) · Washington Times/AP (right, T2) · Trading Economics (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary. Battle damage to the warships is unconfirmed either way. The transit count traces to one Kpler dataset, so it is a single-tracker figure, and the disabled-versus-destroyed split to Marisks via gCaptain alone. Monday's session high was near $97.9, just short of the 24 July peak of $97.93; the move above $98 is Tuesday's.

2 · AfD wins German state election with record 43.8 percent

Alternative für Deutschland took 43.8 percent in Saxony-Anhalt on Sunday, 39 of 83 Landtag seats and three short of the 42 needed. Merz's CDU fell 19.9 points to 17.2. Turnout was 77.8 percent against 60.3 in 2021. It is the highest far-right vote share in Germany since 1945 and the AfD's best result anywhere in its 13-year history.

Landeswahlleiter Sachsen-Anhalt (primary, T1) · wahlrecht.de (T1) · NPR (left, T1) · CNN (left, T1) · France 24 (center, T1) · Washington Examiner (right, T2) · verified, primary source. Vote share varies by count stage across outlets (43.8 to 44.4 AfD, 17.2 to 18.3 CDU); 43.8 and 17.2 are the preliminary official figures. This is not the AfD's first state-election win: it took Thuringia on 1 September 2024.

3 · US envoys meet Putin, then Zelensky, without a breakthrough

Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in Moscow for over three hours Saturday, then Zelensky in Kyiv Sunday, their first visit there, joined in a second round by the British, French and German national security advisers. No agreement was announced. Zelensky told Axios the envoys said Russia is ready to negotiate; he still expects the war to continue into winter.

Kyiv Independent (center, T1) · Al Jazeera (center-left, T1) · NPR (left, T1) · CNN (left, T1) · RFE/RL (center, T2) · Axios interview, 7 September (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary. The "ready to negotiate" line comes from the 7 September Axios interview, not from the 6 September Kyiv talks, and is attributed accordingly.
By region

North America · Canada's C$27.6 billion counter-tariffs take effect

From 00:01 Tuesday Canada applied 15, 25 and 50 percent duties to more than 700 US products worth C$27.6 billion, about $20 billion: steel, aluminium, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, paper, electronics. Steel and aluminium doubled to 50 percent, matching Washington's 22 August tariffs dollar for dollar.

Department of Finance Canada (primary, T1) · CNBC (center, T1) · Washington Post (left, T1) · NPR (left, T1) · The Hill (center, T2) · verified, primary source. C$27.6 billion converts to roughly $20 billion, so the reciprocity is arithmetic rather than rhetoric. Two G7 economies are now in live reciprocal escalation, repricing inputs European buyers compete for.

Asia and Pacific · Anak Krakatau eruption closed Jakarta airport for two days

Continuous eruption from late Friday ran about 25 hours, ash to 15,000 metres. Eight airports including Soekarno-Hatta closed, disrupting about 2,961 flights and stranding roughly 341,000 passengers, before Jakarta reopened early Tuesday. The volcano stays at alert level three of four, with no imminent tsunami risk per the disaster agency.

Reuters (center, T1) · BBC (center, T1) · Al Jazeera (center-left, T1) · Jakarta Globe (center, T2) · Jakarta Post (center, T2) · FlightGlobal (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary. The 1,558-flight and 270,000-passenger figures were the 6 September ministry snapshot and have been superseded.

Asia · Himalayan flood dead pass 1,380, missing above 5,500

Nepal's disaster agency reported 1,341 bodies recovered and about 4,996 missing Sunday; Xinhua reported 43 dead and 519 missing in Tibet. Four people have been found alive since Friday, three in hydropower tunnels. The missing count is up about a quarter in a week.

Nepal NDRRMA via Al Jazeera (center-left, T2) · ABC News/AP (center, T1) · CNN (left, T1) · Washington Times/AP (right, T2) · verified, reliable secondary. The Associated Press reports China tightly restricting information on its side, so the true scale is still unknown. Nepal and Tibet figures are separately attributed; the combined totals are sums reported by outlets, not one authority's figure, and no "deadliest on record" framing is used because no reliable source supports it.
Energy

1 · Jizan refinery struck again; US diesel sets a record $5.90

Saudi Aramco's 400,000 barrel-a-day Jizan refinery was hit Monday, at least the fourth strike since late July. AAA's US retail diesel average set a nominal record $5.9015 on 7 September. Ukrainian drones struck Ryazan on Sunday. Each fresh outage lands on distillate, the barrel Europe imports most.

AAA Gas Prices (primary, T1) · Fox Business (right, T2) · Financial Times originating report via OilPrice (center, T1 originator) · Kyiv Independent (center, T2) · Kyiv Post (center, T2) · verified, primary source for the diesel record; single source, unconfirmed for the Jizan strike, which traces to one Financial Times report citing two people with knowledge, republished by three carriers. Nobody has claimed the strike and the Houthis are suspected. The record is nominal, not inflation-adjusted, which is how AAA keeps it.

2 · TTF at a three-and-a-half-year high, EU storage 67 percent full

Dutch TTF front month traded about €73.6 per megawatt hour Tuesday, the highest in more than three and a half years and up about 21 percent on the month. EU storage stood near 67 percent full on the latest reported gas day, well below the seasonal average. QatarEnergy's force majeure runs into November.

Trading Economics (center, T2) · Gas Outlook reading Gas Infrastructure Europe AGSI+ (center, T2) · Euronews (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary. No point gap to the five-year norm is stated: trackers put that norm anywhere from about 70 to 88 percent for this date. No storage mandate figure is cited either, as it is variously reported as 80 percent or flexible around 75. AGSI publishes the previous gas day, so the fill figure is dated to the latest reported day, not to today.

3 · OPEC+ freezes October quotas at 31.01 million barrels a day

Seven OPEC+ producers agreed Sunday to hold October required output at September levels, 31.01 million barrels a day: Saudi Arabia 10.478 million, Russia 9.949 million, Iraq 4.431 million. It is the group's first pause after four consecutive monthly increases; next review 4 October. No producer-side lever now points at the price.

OPEC statement via The Tribune (primary text, T1) · CNBC (center, T1) · Rigzone (center, T2) · Reuters (center, T1) · verified, primary source. The seven published quotas sum to exactly 31.010, which settles the seven-versus-eight participant question outlets disagreed on.
Economy

1 · Fed hike a coin flip plus, with US CPI on Friday

Traders price a 25 basis point Fed hike on 16 September at roughly 50 to 65 percent as of the 4 September close, Monday having been Labor Day. Treasury's 4 September par yields: two-year 4.13, ten-year 4.78, thirty-year 5.25. August CPI lands Friday. The 112 basis point two-to-thirty gap is the market demanding a fatter term premium, extra compensation for lending long, so a weak long auction this week would raise long borrowing costs worldwide.

US Treasury daily par yield curve (primary, T1) · CNBC (center, T1) · Forbes (right-of-center, T2) · Investing.com (center, T2) · verified, primary source for the yields; the hike probability ships as a range because readings of the same CME tool ran 50, 58.7 and 60 percent, against 70 on 3 September and 66 on 31 August.

2 · ECB set to hike into a shrinking German industry

German industrial output fell 1.1 percent in July, down 1.6 percent year on year and well below consensus. All 65 economists in Reuters' poll expect the ECB to raise its deposit rate to 2.50 percent on Thursday, with euro area inflation at 3.3 percent in August. The ECB is tightening into a shrinking industrial base because the inflation comes from energy supply, not European demand, which is the one thing a rate rise cannot fix.

Destatis via FXStreet (primary release, T1) · Bloomberg (center, T1) · RTE (center, T2) · Euronews (center, T2) · Euractiv (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary. Three different consensus figures circulate for the month, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 percent, so the miss is stated without a number. An auto-sector figure of minus 9.2 percent and an August energy-inflation figure of plus 14.3 percent were both cut: neither appears in the cited releases.

3 · Japan data locks in a BOJ hike, yen at a seven-month high

Japan's Q2 GDP was revised up to an annualised 1.4 percent from 1.1, still short of the 1.6 percent consensus. July real wages rose 2.4 percent, the biggest gain since 2021. The yen strengthened through 154 per dollar, its strongest since 23 February, and a 25 basis point hike on 18 September is largely priced. Japan is the world's cheapest source of borrowed money, so a hike lifts the floor under every long-dated yield.

Cabinet Office second preliminary estimate (primary, T1) · Reuters via Investing.com (center, T1) · MUFG Research (T2) · Nikkei Asia (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary. No destination rate is stated, since only the 25 basis point move itself is confirmed as priced. The yen is given as a direction through 154 rather than a single quote, because a stale 154.29 print contradicted the same-day record.
Politics

1 · Swedish race inside the margin, five days out

Novus for TV4, fielded 1 to 5 September among 2,888 voters, put the centre-left bloc at 50.3 percent against the Tidö parties' 47.9. The Liberals rose a statistically significant 1.0 point to 4.3 percent, though that level is still within the margin of error of the four percent threshold. Clearing four percent is worth roughly fifteen Riksdag seats to the governing bloc instead of zero.

Novus own release (primary, T1) · Demoskop own release, fielded 25 August to 1 September, n=1,998 (primary, T1) · Reuters via US News (center, T1) · verified, primary source. Novus and Demoskop are not merged: different field dates, and their bloc totals are not comparable either (Novus's sum to 98.2, Demoskop's to 88.1, with a different right-bloc definition), so their 2.4 and 3.3 point gaps are not like for like.

2 · The AfD's path to power needs more than one abstention

Saxony-Anhalt's minister-president is elected by secret ballot needing an absolute majority in the first two rounds; if the Landtag then declines to dissolve itself, a third ballot needs only a simple majority of votes cast. The AfD holds 39 seats, the new BSW five, and the remaining 39 refuse to govern with it, so BSW abstention plus one further abstention or defection would install Ulrich Siegmund. The government's term ends 6 October.

Handelsblatt on the ballot rules (center-right, T1) · Time (left, T1) · Euronews (center, T1) · France 24 (center, T1) · verified, reliable secondary. Merz called it the CDU's most severe defeat in years, said consequences must follow, and insisted he will not change course. A Forsa figure of 78 percent saying he underperforms was dropped for want of field dates and sample size. The arithmetic was recomputed: five abstentions leave 78 votes cast and a threshold of 40, so 39 AfD seats still lose without a further defection.

3 · Supreme Court asked to decide two midterm election-mechanics cases

The administration asked the justices on Thursday to revive a Postal Service rule limiting mail-ballot delivery to voters on state-supplied lists; Judge Indira Talwani had blocked it, and 23 states plus Washington DC oppose. Missouri filed separately Friday night to restore a Republican congressional map its state Supreme Court barred from use pending a referendum; Kavanaugh ordered responses by noon Monday.

SCOTUSblog on both applications (center, T1) · Missouri Attorney General's filing, Hoskins v. von Glahn, docket 26A304 (primary, T1) · NBC News (left, T1) · NPR and KCUR (left, T1) · verified, primary source. No requested decision date is stated: outlets give 13 or 14 September and SCOTUSblog gives none, and no ballot-printing deadline is claimed because no cited source states one.

Fixed by the Fact-checker before publication, twelve corrections on sixteen checks and the heaviest pass yet. The top line's claim that the AfD won a state election "for the first time" was false, since Thuringia went to the AfD in September 2024; it was replaced with the true and stronger superlative, and the World item now names Thuringia explicitly. Brent's "intraday high $98.06" was a Tuesday level attached to Monday's session and contradicted its own sentence, so the settle and Tuesday's move are now separated. The missile claim was rewritten so CENTCOM's account and the Revolutionary Guard's claim are both attributed rather than one stated as fact, and the sequence corrected: Iran launched first. Zelensky's "ready to negotiate" quote turned out to be real but from a 7 September Axios interview, not the 6 September Kyiv talks, and none of the five cited sources carried it. The Krakatau item was stale in both directions at once, with the airports already reopened while the disruption totals had nearly doubled. The flood item's "missing count has roughly doubled in a week" was disproved by this paper's own archive, which puts the rise at about a quarter, and an unsourced "deadliest on record" was cut. Jizan's strike count was corrected from the second in a month to at least the fourth since late July. The gas item lost a phantom range end no tracker gave and a point gap to a five-year norm that swings by methodology. The Fed probability was widened and pinned to Thursday's close, because Monday was Labor Day and a same-day reading was not a live market print. Two unsourced German figures were cut. Japan's yen quote was disproved by its own source's headline. The Swedish poll item was corrected to say what Novus actually certified, the change rather than the level. The AfD coalition arithmetic was recomputed and now requires a further abstention or defection beyond the BSW's five seats. Merz's quote was softened from "in decades" to "in years", his actual words. And the Supreme Court item was corrected on the filing date, on "voided" versus barred from use pending a referendum, and by dropping an unsourced ballot-printing deadline.
