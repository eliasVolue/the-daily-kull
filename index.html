1 · The World

1 · US proposes new tariffs on the EU, China and 12 others

On 25 November the US Trade Representative proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies over industrial overcapacity: 15 percent on the EU, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland, up to 20 percent extra on China and up to 35 on Vietnam. Hearings start 25 January.

USTR press release, 25 November (T1); CNBC citing Reuters (center, T2); Bloomberg and BBC (center, T2); headlines at The Guardian, The New York Times and The Washington Post (left) and The Wall Street Journal and The Times (right). Section 301 lets the US retaliate against foreign practices it judges unfair; it replaces the emergency tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February. The EU's 15 percent includes existing duties, matching its deal cap. Reports say China's rate could double without a deal; Vietnam's drops to 20 with one. Comments due 5 January; no effective date; Mexico and Norway undecided. Verification: verified, primary source.

2 · AI sell-off returns as US inflation runs hot

At about 15:50 CET Wednesday, intraday, the S&P 500 was down 3 percent, the Nasdaq 4.3 and Nvidia 8.7, after Tuesday's 0.5 percent fall to 7,201.09. Korea's Kospi closed down 6.2 percent. The ten year Treasury yield fell to 4.83 percent from 4.91.

Bloomberg via BNN (center, T2); headline at The Wall Street Journal (right); FRED SP500 series (T1, Tuesday close); US Treasury daily par yield curve (T1, 4.91 on 24 November); Al Jazeera (center, T2, Kospi); intraday levels rechecked against one minute market data by the Fact-checker. At the 16:30 cutoff the S&P was down 2.6 percent, the Nasdaq 4.0 and Nvidia 7.7. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · Houthis claim strike on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery

The Houthis claimed a drone strike on Ras Tanura, a 550,000 barrel a day refinery beside Saudi Arabia's main export terminal. The coalition says it downed four drones and has not confirmed a hit; NASA imagery reviewed by Reuters showed about 600 square kilometres of smoke.

Reuters via Business Recorder and The National (center, T2); CNBC (center, T2); Al Jazeera, DW and France 24 (center, T2). The smoke figure appears in one Reuters carrier only. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claim two more tankers hit mines on 25 November; that is Iran's claim only. Verification: verified, reliable secondary (claim and interceptions); damage unconfirmed.

2 · By region

Europe · US drops Russia business incentives for "land for peace"

The Financial Times, citing five officials, reported on 25 November that Washington has shelved energy and aviation deals offered to Russia and is pivoting to territory for peace, chiefly the Donbas, aiming for a deal by early January.

Financial Times (center, T2), as carried by Reuters, US News, Ukrainska Pravda and Meduza (T2). Every carrier traces to the one FT report. Verification: single source, unconfirmed.

Latin America · Venezuela puts its debt at 237.5 billion dollars

Venezuela's first macroeconomic framework in years puts public debt at 237.5 billion dollars, 242 percent of 2025 GDP, calls it unsustainable and promises a restructuring covering all creditors. Its bonds rose over 4 cents.

Reuters via Yahoo Finance (center, T2, 16:24 CET on 24 November); Bloomberg (center, T2); TimesLive (T2). Published six minutes before the window opened and not carried last issue. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

Asia and Pacific · Australia and Solomon Islands treaty bars foreign bases

A treaty signed in Canberra on 25 November bars any foreign military base in the Solomon Islands, names Australia primary security partner and commits 740 million Australian dollars over a decade; Honiara reportedly ends its 2022 China pact.

AP via NBC News (center, T2); Politico (center, T2); Al Jazeera (center, T2); ABC Australia (center, T2); The New York Times (left, T2). The China pact's end rests on Australian media reports. Not yet in force: Solomons legislation goes to parliament in January or February. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · Southern Norway's reservoirs below their 20-year minimum

NVE's week 47 data put NO2 reservoirs at 50.8 percent, against a 2006 to 2025 median of 80.0 and minimum of 57.3; Norway overall 66.0 percent against 78.6. A deficit this deep keeps southern Sweden tied to continental prices this winter.

NVE reservoir statistics, week 47, published 25 November (T1); Adressa (center, T2, national figure). The last sentence is the desk's analysis. Verification: verified, primary source.

3 · Brent rebounds to 91.40 dollars on Gulf attacks

January Brent settled at 91.40 dollars on 24 November, up 2.03, and traded at 92.22 at 13:32 CET Wednesday, intraday, after the Ras Tanura claim. Iraq is disputing its OPEC+ capacity baseline before ministers meet on 29 November.

Reuters via Forbes Middle East and The Globe and Mail (center, T2); market data (T2, settlement); Reuters and ING (center, T2, Iraq's dispute). February Brent trades about 3 dollars lower. Verification: verified, reliable secondary (settlement); single source, unconfirmed (Wednesday's quote).

4 · Economy

1 · US core inflation beats forecasts; growth revised up

October PCE prices, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, rose 3.8 percent on a year earlier; core rose 3.3 percent against 3.2 expected. Third quarter growth was revised to 4.3 percent annualised from 3.4. Futures fully price a Fed rise on 9 December.

BEA personal income and outlays, 25 November (T1); Reuters, CNBC and The Hill (center, T2); Fox Business (right, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · German growth cut to 0.1 percent in the third quarter

Destatis cut third quarter growth to 0.1 percent from the 0.2 flash; machinery and equipment investment fell 3.5 percent and exports 1.0. Employment was 222,000 lower than a year earlier.

Destatis press release No. 409, 25 November (T1); Newsquawk (center, T2, consensus). Verification: verified, primary source.

3 · Swedish producer prices up 11.8 percent on the year

SCB: producer prices rose 0.9 percent in October and 11.8 percent on a year earlier, from 11.2; energy related goods rose 41.1 percent and import prices 17.6. Consumer inflation is 0.9 percent, so the pressure sits upstream.

SCB statistical news, 25 November (T1); Newsquawk (center, T2). CPIF, the Riksbank's target measure, is 1.1 percent. Verification: verified, primary source.

5 · Politics

1 · Sweden: Kristersson faces a Riksdag vote on Monday

Speaker Andreas Norlén nominated Ulf Kristersson on 24 November to lead a Moderate, Christian Democrat, Liberal and Centre government; the Riksdag votes 30 November at 13:00, the third vote. Norlén called the risk of an extra election substantial.

Riksdagen.se (T1); SVT Nyheter and Sveriges Radio (center, T2); TT and TV4 (center, T2); headlines at Dagens Nyheter (left) and Svenska Dagbladet (right). If this vote fails, one attempt remains before an extra election. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · Centre Party divided as Monday's vote nears

The Centre Party is split over backing Kristersson; leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist reportedly told members the choice is Kristersson or an extra election. Sveriges Radio's Indikator poll puts the party at 4.8 percent, close to the 4 percent threshold.

Sveriges Radio Ekot (center, T1 for its own poll); SVT Nyheter (center, T2, the leader's framing from unnamed sources); Svenska Dagbladet (right, T2, the internal split); headline at Dagens Nyheter (left). Verification: verified, reliable secondary (poll: verified, primary source).

3 · Israel: Eisenkot seals the first coalition deal

Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar agreed a coalition deal with Avigdor Liberman's Yisrael Beytenu on 24 November and sealed it on 25 November; the Democrats suspended talks over his unity talks with Likud. His mandate runs to 6 December, extendable to 20 December.

Times of Israel and Ynet (center, T2); Bloomberg and Al Jazeera (center, T2); headline at The Washington Post (left). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.
