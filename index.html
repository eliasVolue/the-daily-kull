1 · The World

1 · The US ten-year breaks 5 percent, the first time since 2007

The benchmark yield crossed 5 percent during Monday's New York morning, reaching the wires minutes before this window opened, and held above it through Tuesday. Monday's official close was 4.97 percent and the thirty-year 5.34 percent.

US Treasury daily par yield curve, fetched directly (primary, T1; the Federal Reserve H.15 series carries the same Treasury data, so it is one issuer and not a second source); Federal Reserve Economic Data DGS10 and DGS30 full history (primary, T1); CNN Business (left, T1); Fox Business (right, T1); Bloomberg (center, T1); Trading Economics (center, T3, for the 2007 superlative only). Verification: verified, primary source. Three repairs: a desk wrote “late Monday” for a crossing that happened mid-morning New York time, quoted an intraday 5.02 to 5.04 percent that neither closing source supports, and compared it to the October 2023 peak of 4.98, which is a close and so not a like-for-like comparator. Tuesday's own close of 5.00 percent, the first close at or above 5 since 19 July 2007, printed about five hours after this issue's cutoff and is therefore the next issue's fact.

2 · Saudi Arabia's only large Hormuz bypass is shut a fifth day

The East-West pipeline, which moves 4 to 5 million barrels a day to the Red Sea, was still offline on Tuesday, the fifth day since Friday's drone strike. Goldman Sachs put repairs between “very soon” and eight weeks; regional officials told the Associated Press three to five weeks.

Reuters wire, read in full via BNN Bloomberg (center, T1); Associated Press, via Middle East Eye and Politico (center, T1); CNBC (center, T1); CNN Business (left, T1); Al Jazeera (center, T2); Washington Times (right, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Kpler's crude analyst Johannes Rauball puts Yanbu's tank cover at roughly 15 million barrels, about four days of withdrawals; a competing estimate of five to seven days is in circulation, so the figure is carried as one tracking firm's and not as fact. One thing sits just outside the window and cuts against the eight-week end of the range: US Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC at 18:15 CEST that the closure is a brief interruption lasting days.

3 · Iran rules out talks as Hormuz traffic thins to four vessels

Security council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said there are no talks until Iran's conditions are met, rebuffing Trump's Monday opening. Kpler counted four commodity vessels through the strait on Monday, down from ten, on preliminary data.

Reuters wire body, read in full (center, T1); Agence France-Presse via the Oman Observer (center, T2); Press TV and Tasnim, Iranian state, for the statement itself (T3, claim attribution only); Democracy Now (left, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Two repairs: a desk wrote “four vessels through the strait” when the wire's number is commodity vessel traffic specifically, and omitted that the Kpler data is preliminary. His statement first moved on Iranian state media at 23:01 CEST Monday, which is after midnight in Tehran, so both “Monday evening” and “Tuesday” are defensible; it is inside the window either way.

Two Italian Eurofighters on the Baltic air policing mission destroyed a drone about 100 kilometres west of Vilnius, near the Kaunas Reservoir, shortly after midnight. Lithuanian officials called it the first shootdown in Lithuanian airspace and did not comment on the drone's origin.

Associated Press wire, read in full via NPR (wire center, T1; NPR left, T1); Guardian (left, T1); Deutsche Welle (center, T2); Bloomberg (center, T1); Washington Times (right, T2); Fox News (right, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Three repairs, all about attribution. A desk wrote “about 95 kilometres”, which matches no source and is the midpoint of the Associated Press's own two figures, 90 kilometres northwest in its photo caption and about 100 kilometres west in its attributed body sentence; the body figure ships. A desk also moved a Belarus origin onto the crisis centre's authority, when the same copy says explicitly that authorities declined to comment on origin or purpose pending investigation, and outlets split three ways between suspected Russian, suspected Belarusian and unidentified. Europe, the Netherlands · Rail sabotage at 30 locations halts the morning rush

Long pipes fastened to the rails and jumper cables bridging track sections at 30 locations made signals read the track as occupied. A train struck one near Steenwijk with no injuries. Services were restarting by about 14:08 CEST. Nobody has claimed responsibility.

ProRail and Nederlandse Spoorwegen statements (primary, T1); Bloomberg (center, T1); Associated Press via ABC News (center, T1); Euronews (center, T2); Railfreight (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Four repairs: “more than 30 places” overstates ProRail's own count of 30, “halts the network” was stale by press time because trains were restarting inside the window, an unsourced 05:30 first discovery was cut, and “coordinated” was cut as an inference, since what is established is sabotage material at 30 locations with nobody identified. It fell on Budget Day and the state opening of parliament, with farmer protests underway; do not attribute it to Russia, because no investigator has tied it to any state, and both Extinction Rebellion and Agractie denied involvement.

What being late does to a newspaper. A catch-up run has one failure mode above all others, and the Fact-checker named it precisely: a live dashboard read on the wrong day. A page that shows a current value with no date will happily hand you Wednesday's number for a Tuesday issue, and it happened three times in one draft. The Swedish nuclear total of 3,053 megawatts is Wednesday's reactor-register reading, not Tuesday's output of 3,310. The British ten-year at 5.36 was checked against a vendor page updated Wednesday. The Federal Reserve hike probability of 92 percent is Wednesday's phrasing on a vendor page, where the in-window range was 87 to 89. All three are repaired, and the rule is now recorded in every desk memory: on a late run, any figure from a page showing a current value rather than a dated series must be re-derived for the issue date. Two techniques did the re-deriving. A vendor's daily-change field dates a reading for free, because level minus change is the previous session, and that settled the Turkish, Greek and British yields. And news wire timestamps in Greenwich Mean Time are the whole window check, remembering that a 16:30 CEST cutoff is 14:30 GMT.
