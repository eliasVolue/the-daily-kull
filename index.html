1 · The World

Brent's session high was $109.78 at 14:40 CEST, about 4 percent above Friday's $104.61 close, with WTI at $103.88 by 16:30. The US ten-year traded near 4.99 percent and European equities fell across the board, the Euro Stoxx 50 down about 2.8 percent.

2 · The Hormuz talks are postponed indefinitely, at Saudi Arabia's request

Oman confirmed that Monday's Salalah meeting of Gulf foreign ministers and Iran is postponed with no new date. Iranian officials say Saudi Arabia asked for the delay over Houthi attacks on its own territory; Oman cited the need for conducive conditions. Saudi Arabia is reported to have objected to the draft text and Bahrain to have refused to attend.

3 · Washington says the Saudi pipeline returns soon, while Riyadh says nothing

US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Monday that the East-West pipeline, shut since Friday, should be running again soon, giving no timetable. Three days on, neither Saudi Arabia nor Aramco has published a damage estimate or a repair date.

Bloomberg Television interview, carried onward (center, T1); CNBC (center, T1); NBC News (center-left, T2). Verification: single source, unconfirmed. Wright's remarks trace to one interview with several carriers, not to independent reporting, so the label is mandatory. Two repairs: a desk wrote “five days on” when the shutdown was Friday and today is Monday, and the satellite images showing fire damage at a pumping station were released on Sunday by Vantor through Getty, not published today, even though the article surfacing them is dated today. The capacity figure of 7 million barrels a day, cut this morning as one trade-press outlier against 4 to 5 million from every T1 carrier and the Energy Information Administration, was reinstated by a desk this afternoon and stays cut.

2 · By region

Middle East · Houthi forces take the Hanish islands beside the Bab el-Mandeb approach

Houthi forces captured Greater and Lesser Hanish on Monday, about 160 kilometres north of Bab el-Mandeb, after hundreds of government-allied forces withdrew. It follows last week's capture of Mokha and Mayun and tightens the group's hold on the route now substituting for a disrupted Hormuz.

Associated Press wire, read in full (center, T1); Euronews (center, T2); Washington Times (right, T3). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. One repair, and it matters: a desk called the withdrawing force UAE-backed, but the same Associated Press copy states that the United Arab Emirates dismantled its military infrastructure on the islands earlier this year after falling out with Saudi Arabia and the Yemeni government, so the label was already out of date at the moment described. The wire's own sourcing is two government officials and a Houthi official.

Europe · Tusk convenes his security chiefs and will consult NATO on the train strike

Tusk convened ministers and security chiefs on Monday over the Russian drone that hit the Kyiv to Warsaw train at Yahodyn, saying he would consult NATO allies on a joint response and that a Russian strike on Poland is now a real possibility. Rutte said the strikes would push NATO to increase support for Kyiv.

Associated Press wire (center, T1); Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty (center, T2); Kyiv Post (center, T2); Washington Times (right, T3). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. This morning the desk cut a Tusk security meeting because no source carried it. The Fact-checker was asked specifically whether this is the same unsourced claim returning with a carrier attached, and found it is not: the meeting is independently reported and dated Monday 14 September.

North America · Carney rejects EU membership on the record and seeks a “unique alliance”

Carney said Monday that Canada is “not looking to become a member” of the European Union but will pursue a “unique alliance”, after the Wall Street Journal reported he was exploring associate member status. Poilievre said Carney “could not be more out of touch”.

Wall Street Journal, originating report (right, T1); CBC News (center, T1); BNN Bloomberg (center, T2); CTV News (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Poilievre's quote is restored to its full form, because as the desk compressed it the attack read as aimed at the idea rather than at Carney.

3 · Sweden's nationwide recount begins, separate from Wednesday's late count

Valmyndigheten confirmed that the county administrative boards began recounting every election-night ballot on Monday, checking validity and both party and personal votes. This is a different count from Wednesday's late and overseas votes. The final official result is still expected the weekend of 19 to 20 September, with the blocs at 176 to 173.

Valmyndigheten (primary, T1); Sveriges Television (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. Worth the slot because the two counts are routinely described as one: the recount running now can move seats, and Wednesday's is a separate exercise on a separate set of ballots.

Why there are two editions today. The catchup task built this morning after Friday's failure did exactly what it was built to do: it fired at 09:00, found no issue for Friday 11 September, and shipped the weekend special at 11:34. That is the first time the safety net has caught a real miss. It also means the 16:30 scheduled run arrived to find today's paper already on the page, so under the standing rerun rule this is a second edition of the same issue rather than a new one: the same archive file, the same link, the morning edition preserved in the archive rather than overwritten.

What the Fact-checker changed. Four fixes, no blocks. The largest is a pattern rather than a single error: all three desks that quoted Brent this afternoon gave a number between $108.90 and $109.01 and presented it as the day's high, when the actual session high was $109.78 at 14:40 and those figures were the press-time level. This is the same conflation the desk was corrected for this morning on Dutch gas, twice in one day, so the price story is now split deliberately across two items, one carrying the significance and one the shape of the day. The Saudi pipeline item lost two claims: “five days on” when the shutdown was Friday and today is Monday, and satellite images described as published today when they were released on Sunday by Vantor through Getty, the article surfacing them being the thing that is new. The Hanish islands item called the withdrawing force UAE-backed when the same wire copy states the United Arab Emirates dismantled its military infrastructure there earlier this year, so the label was stale at the moment described. The Tusk security meeting cut this morning as unsourced was specifically re-examined and is real, independently reported and dated today, which is the right outcome: the morning was right to cut it and this afternoon is right to run it.

Two things the desks got right that are worth recording. The Tuesday Nord Pool figures reproduced to the cent when the Fact-checker recomputed all 96 quarter-hourly values independently. And the ten-year superlative, which two desks phrased differently, turned out not to be a conflict at all: the Federal Reserve's own series puts the last close at today's level on 19 October 2023, so “highest since October 2023” and “three-year high” are the same fact at different precision.

Killed this edition: US diesel at a record $6.23 a gallon, because it already shipped in this morning's edition and is not new. A “Wall Street falls as Europe rises” item, on a direct conflict: one desk had the DAX and Euro Stoxx 50 up about 0.8 percent, while the data desk measured them down, and the Fact-checker's independent pull gave the DAX at −2.58 percent, the CAC 40 at −2.74, the Euro Stoxx 50 at −2.76 and the OMXS30 at −1.11.
