Top line

1 · The World

1 · Nvidia's 31 percent jump lifts Wall Street, then fades

On 23 November the S&P 500 closed up 4.7 percent at 7,237.17 and the Nasdaq 6.5 percent as Nvidia rose 31.5 percent on Huang's reinstatement, which Trump says he brokered. On Tuesday Nvidia was down 6.6 percent at 16:05 CET, intraday.

AP closing table via La Nación (center, T2); Yahoo chart data (T2); FRED Dow Jones series (T1); The Guardian and The Washington Post (left, T2); CNBC and NBC (center, T2, Trump's role); AP, 24 November (center, T2, Tuesday). Nvidia's best day in 23 years and, at about 952 billion dollars, a record one-day gain in market value for any stock (MarketWatch). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · Russia warns Estonia of open war; NATO sends AWACS to Poland

Russia said on 23 November Estonia was on the verge of open war over closing its land border crossings from 1 December; Tallinn denied letting its territory be used against Russia. NATO will station an AWACS surveillance aircraft in Poland.

Reuters via RTÉ (center, T2); Meduza (T2); Time and CNBC (center, T2); ERR, Estonia's public broadcaster (center, T2); Reuters, NBC and Notes from Poland (center, T2, AWACS); headline at The Washington Post (left). The quoted phrase varies by translation. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · Houthi strike hits Riyadh's financial district; Iran claims mined tankers

A Houthi attack hit a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District on 24 November, injuring 11, Saudi civil defence said, the second strike there in a week. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claim five tankers hit mines in Hormuz; nothing independent confirms it.

Al Jazeera, Anadolu, Arab News and Al-Monitor (center, T2, all relying on Saudi statements); Times of Israel (T2); Marine Insight and CNBC (T2, mine claims unverified); The Wall Street Journal (right, T2) reports a US probe into suspected Iranian cyberattacks on up to 50 tankers. Verification: verified, reliable secondary (Riyadh); the mine strikes are Iran's claim only.

2 · By region

Europe · Zelensky confirms Ukraine struck Russian ship off Finland

Zelensky told Bloomberg that Ukraine hit the Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland on 21 November, calling it a weapons carrier of a Russian defence ministry logistics company. Finland closed nearby air and sea areas.

Bloomberg (center, T2); Reuters (center, T2); Kyiv Independent (center, T2); Yle (center, T2); Daily Sabah (right, T2). The ship's owner is Zelensky's account, not independently confirmed. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

Asia and Pacific · China extends mineral export pause to 27 January

China on 24 November extended its suspension of the export ban to the US on gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials from 27 November to 27 January 2027, past the US-China truce's 10 January expiry.

Reuters (center, T2); Bloomberg (center, T2); Financial Times (center, T2); Anadolu (center, T2, citing the commerce ministry notice). Licensing still applies. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

North America · Pentagon calls Canada a failing defence partner

A Pentagon fact sheet on 23 November said Canada is failing as a credible partner in defending North America, citing underinvestment, spending rises deferred until after the 2029 election, and Ottawa's review of its F-35 purchase.

AP via ABC News (center, T2); Euronews (center, T2); The Washington Times (right, T2). The fact sheet may have been released before this window opened; the last issue did not carry it. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · Energy

1 · Brent at a three-month low, then rebounds

January Brent settled at 89.37 dollars on 23 November, down 3.5 percent, as Libya's Sharara pipeline reopened; IEA data put Gulf exports in October at 74 percent of pre-war levels. On Tuesday it traded at 90.55 at 15:38 CET, intraday.

Reuters (center, T2, three-month low); Yahoo futures data and Nation Thailand (T2, settlement); CNBC (center, T2, Tuesday's quote); IEA monthly report, 13 November (T1); headline at The Wall Street Journal (right). The February contract trades about 3 dollars lower; OPEC+ sets 2027 output on 29 November. Verification: verified, reliable secondary (settlement); single source, unconfirmed (Tuesday's quote).

2 · SE3 day-ahead rises to 218 euros for Wednesday; Ringhals 4 back

Wednesday's day-ahead averages 218.50 euros per MWh in SE3, up from 191.40; SE4 268.93, above Germany's 246.29; SE2 95.10; NO1 227.82; FI 137.86. Ringhals 4 is producing again and ramping to full output.

energy-charts.info day-ahead, daily average of all 96 quarter-hours, recomputed by the desk and again by the Fact-checker (T1; Finland on its own delivery day, desk figure only); Vattenfall and SVT Halland, 24 November (T1 and center, T2, Ringhals 4). Verification: verified, primary source.

3 · Kraftlyftet's first awards: 1.1 billion kronor for SE3 and SE4

On 23 November the Swedish Energy Agency made its first Kraftlyftet awards: about 1.1 billion kronor to six companies, including Fortum, E.ON and Vattenfall, for roughly 100 MW of dispatchable capacity in SE3 and SE4, mostly biomass combined heat and power.

Swedish Energy Agency release, 23 November (T1); E.ON release via TT (T1); Energinyheter (T3, biomass share). Announced Monday morning, before this window; carried now because the last issue held it on sourcing. The full call is 4.2 billion kronor. Verification: verified, primary source.

4 · Economy

1 · A Fed rise on 9 December is priced as near certain

New York Fed president John Williams said on 23 November the Fed still has work to do to bring inflation back to 2 percent; futures price a rise of at least a quarter point on 9 December as near certain. The ten year Treasury closed at 4.92 percent.

US Treasury daily par yield curve (T1); CNBC (center, T2, Williams); Reuters and Bloomberg (center, T2); CME FedWatch (T2: about 93.5 percent on a quarter point, 6.5 on half a point). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · US consumer confidence jumps to highest since January 2025

The Conference Board's index rose to 103.3 in November from a revised 91.9, against about 92.5 expected, its highest since January 2025, as uncertainty eased after the midterms, the Board said, led by stronger views of the labour market.

Conference Board release, 24 November (T1); AP (center, T2); headline at Reuters (center). The consensus figure rests on one source. Verification: verified, primary source.

3 · German business mood rises for a seventh month

Germany's Ifo business climate rose for a seventh straight month, to 90.6 in November from 90.1, against 90.5 expected; expectations rose to 91.9. Services improved while manufacturing and construction fell; Ifo said the recovery lacks breadth.

ifo release, 24 November (T1); Reuters via RTÉ (center, T2); FXStreet (T3, consensus). Verification: verified, primary source.

5 · Politics

1 · Sweden: Andersson gives up; Kristersson asks to be tested

Magdalena Andersson told Speaker Andreas Norlén on 24 November she cannot form a government. Ulf Kristersson said he is ready to be tested leading Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals and Centre; the Centre Party has not decided.

SVT Nyheter and Sveriges Radio (center, T2); TT (center, T2); Kvartal and Svenska Dagbladet (right, T2); headline at Dagens Nyheter (left). The Sweden Democrats say they will sit in opposition and tolerate him. Whether the Speaker named a candidate at 16:30 is left for Wednesday. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · US Senate Republicans weigh curbing Iran war funding

Senate appropriations chair Susan Collins is exploring a bill that restocks Pentagon munitions without paying for ongoing operations against Iran; majority leader John Thune signals willingness to negotiate. Government funding lapses 11 December.

The Washington Post, 23 November (left, T2); The Hill (center, T2). Democrats demand an outright bar on offensive operations; Punchbowl News (center, T2, single source) reports the White House is preparing for a stopgap to 31 March. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · UK: Burnham rules out rejoining the EU without a referendum

Prime Minister Andy Burnham told Parliament on 23 November it would be unthinkable for Britain to rejoin the EU without a referendum, while promoting last week's UK-EU deal; Bloomberg reports cabinet ministers were surprised by his backing for a possible vote.

RTÉ carrying PA and Reuters (center, T2, the quote); headlines at The Guardian (left), the Financial Times, BBC and Bloomberg (center) and The Times (right). The Bloomberg report was read as a headline only. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.
