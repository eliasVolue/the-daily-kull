Europe (Sweden) · Election gap narrows further, to 2.6 points

Two differently-sampled Swedish polls (Novus/TV4, Indikator/SR) show the opposition's lead over Kristersson's government narrowing further, to 2.6 and 4.7 points, both down sharply from summer readings, with the 13 September election now inside two weeks.

North America · Canada eases some tariffs, hardens others

Canada removed 25 percent counter-tariffs on CUSMA-compliant US goods from 1 September, while confirming new retaliatory tariffs on C$27.6 billion of non-compliant goods from 8 September, matching Washington's 50 percent tariffs dollar-for-dollar.

Economy

1 · Bond yields spike further on reignited Iran war

The reignited US-Iran war pushed the US 10-year Treasury yield to about 4.81 percent, highest since late 2023; Japan's 10-year held above 3 percent and Germany's bund hit 3.38 percent, highest since 2011.

Bloomberg (center, T1) · CNBC (center, T1) · Fortune (center, T2) · single source, unconfirmed (the US figure is a market quote, not yet Fed H.15-confirmed)

2 · US private payrolls growth stalls before jobs report

ADP reported US private employers added just 38,000 jobs in August, down from 46,000 in July and below the 47,000 forecast, the weakest reading since January; manufacturing shed 17,000 jobs, raising the stakes for Friday's official jobs report.

CNBC (center, T1) · Reuters via The Globe and Mail (center, T1) · Fox Business (right, T2) · verified, primary source

3 · Eurozone factory output hits 51-month high

The HCOB/S&P Global eurozone manufacturing PMI climbed to 52.7 in August from 51.9, the highest since May 2022, with new orders growing fastest since early 2022 and export gains led by Germany, Austria and the Netherlands.

S&P Global (primary, T1) · Investing.com/Reuters (center, T1) · verified, primary source

Sweden, with the Netherlands, Poland and Spain, urged the EU to renew use of roughly 200 billion euros in frozen Russian assets after officials warned the existing 90 billion euro Ukraine loan won't cover 2027.
