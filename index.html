The top line

US payrolls beat revives Fed rate-hike bets as long bond yields hit multi-decade highs from Tokyo to London; Europe's diesel premium tops $100 a barrel for the first time.

The World

1 · US jobs surprise revives Fed hike case; Trump threatens trade cutoff

Payrolls rose 162,000 in August against about 53,000 expected, unemployment steady at 4.1 percent; September Fed hike odds jumped from 49.4 percent a day earlier to between 53 and 60 percent depending on the source. Trump posted on Truth Social, in capitals: "LOWER THE RATE OR I'LL STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE HAVE A DEFICIT."

Bureau of Labor Statistics (primary, T1) · Washington Post (left, T1) · CNN Business (left, T1) · CNBC (center, T1) · Fox Business (right, T2) · verified, reliable secondary — the same-day hike-odds probability is an intraday snapshot, so it ships as a range; four outlets citing the same CME tool gave 52.6, 58.2, 60.4 and "above 60" percent, and all four agree on the prior day's 49.4.

2 · New York Times analysis points to US munition in wedding strike

A New York Times visual analysis, with former US Army ordnance technician Trevor Ball, found debris consistent with a US-made JSOW glide bomb and concluded the Kuhestak wedding strike was likely a direct hit by a US munition. Five were killed and 68 wounded; Vice President Vance said he would not call the conflict a war. The US says the intended target was a telecommunications tower about 135 metres from the wedding house.

New York Times (left, T1) · Associated Press (center wire, T1) · CBS News (center, T2) · CNN (left, T2) · Stars and Stripes (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary

3 · Volkswagen approves 100,000 job cuts, largest auto restructuring ever

Volkswagen's supervisory board approved Future Plan 2030 on 3 September: 50,000 further cuts on top of 50,000 already agreed, roughly 15 percent of global staff, eclipsing General Motors' 50,000 after its 2009 bankruptcy. The model range is cut by up to half by 2035; Hannover, Emden, Zwickau and Neckarsulm have no guarantee beyond 2031-2034.

By region

Asia and Pacific · Two pulled alive from Nepal tunnel nine days after flood

Two men were pulled alive from the Trishuli 3A hydropower tunnel on 4 September, nine days after glacier-collapse floods. Nepali authorities counted about 900 hydropower workers missing and roughly 500 believed trapped underground as of 2 September; the wider flood toll stands at 1,252 dead.

NPR (left, T1) · Reuters (center, T1) · Guardian (left, T2) · NBC News (center, T1) · Nepal Army and NDRRMA (primary, T1) · verified, reliable secondary — the rescue is confirmed across nine outlets, but the missing and trapped counts date to 2 September and could not be re-confirmed today.

Europe · Sweden's Social Democrats hit their lowest poll rating ever

Two polls, one published 3 September and one this morning, put the Social Democrats at their weakest of the campaign: Ekot/Indikator has them at 27.9 percent, the lowest the party has ever recorded, and Aftonbladet/Demoskop at its lowest since 2023. The Liberals sit below the four percent threshold, nine days before the 13 September vote.

Sveriges Radio/Ekot (center, T1) · Indikator Opinion and Demoskop (primary, T1) · SVT Nyheter (center, T1) · Kvartal (right, T2) · Svenska Dagbladet (right, T2) · verified, reliable secondary — Demoskop uses a self-recruited panel and has diverged from random-sample pollsters before, so the two are attributed separately.

Latin America · Milei moves to sanction Falklands oil after Trump wavers

Milei said Argentina will send Congress an urgent sovereignty bill sanctioning suppliers to the Rockhopper-Navitas Sea Lion oilfield, after Trump told GB News he would not commit to defending the Falklands, tying his position to Britain's lack of support in the Iran war. Health secretary Wes Streeting called Britain's commitment absolute and unshakeable.

Energy

1 · Europe's diesel premium tops $100 a barrel for the first time

Europe's diesel crack over Brent passed $100 a barrel on 3 September, a first: diesel is now worth more than twice its underlying crude. US retail diesel hit a record $5.85 a gallon on 4 September on AAA's own national average, beating the June 2022 peak. Russia's fourth-largest refinery, Lukoil's NORSI, and Novatek's Ust-Luga condensate complex both remain offline after Ukrainian strikes.

Financial Times (center, T1) · AAA national average (primary, T1) · Seeking Alpha (center, T2) · NPR (left, T1) · Axios (center, T2) · Ukrainian General Staff (primary, T1) · verified, reliable secondary — the US diesel crack passed $100 back on 18 August, so the "first" holds for Europe specifically.

2 · EU gas storage at its lowest for the season in over a decade

EU storage stood at 65.85 percent full on 3 September on Gas Infrastructure Europe's own data, the lowest for the time of year in over a decade, though the European Commission says it sees no immediate supply risk. TTF spiked to about €75 per megawatt hour, the highest since January 2023, before easing about 3 percent on 4 September.

Gas Infrastructure Europe (primary, T1) · CNBC (center, T1) · Anadolu (center, T2) · OilPrice (center, T2) · verified, primary source for the storage level. The "over a decade" framing varies by tracker: outlets have called the same series lowest since 2011, lowest since 2013 and a five-year low this season, so it is not stated as a record.

3 · Hormuz transits collapse further as trackers disagree on flows

Four commodity vessels crossed Hormuz on 3 September, down from nine the day before, against a ten-day average near 15. Flow estimates diverge sharply: Rystad puts daily oil and product exits mostly between 4 and 6 million barrels, against roughly 20 million before the war. Brent traded around $95, up nearly 8 percent on the week, its strongest week since July.

gCaptain vessel tracking (primary-equivalent, T1) · Rystad via gCaptain (center, T2) · Reuters (center, T1) · Trading Economics (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary — Kpler and Rystad give materially different flow ranges, so each counts as a single source, not corroboration of the other.
Economy

1 · Long bond yields at multi-decade highs from Tokyo to Paris

Japan's 30-year touched about 4.15 percent before Thursday's auction, which then passed smoothly and eased the sell-off, leaving the yield back near 3.97 percent. Britain's 10-year gilt hit a 19-year high of 5.29 percent on 2 September before easing to 5.14; France's 10-year sits at about 4.21 percent, its highest since November 2008.

Bloomberg (center, T1) · Reuters (center, T1) · Trading Economics (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary — Japan's all-time 30-year high is disputed between 4.155 and 4.21 percent, so no record figure is stated here.

2 · US services prices hit a four-year high while services hiring shrinks

ISM's services index rose to 55.4 in August from 54.1, but its prices-paid gauge hit 72.6, the highest in four years, while services employment contracted for a second month at 47.8. Reaccelerating services inflation alongside shrinking services hiring is what makes the Fed's September decision genuinely contested rather than mechanical.

Institute for Supply Management (primary, T1) · Trading Economics ISM series (center, T2) · TradingView (center, T2) · verified, primary source

3 · Europe splits: households retreat while German factories keep recovering

Eurozone retail volumes fell 0.6 percent in July, with Germany the steepest faller at minus 3.4 percent, while German factory orders rose 2.5 percent, driven by large transport-equipment orders. Euro area retail was still up 0.6 percent year on year, so this is a monthly move rather than a trend.

Eurostat (primary, T1) · Destatis via AFP and France 24 (center, T1) · Investing.com (center, T2) · verified, primary source
Politics

1 · Missouri court blocks Republican House map, orders a referendum

Missouri's Supreme Court unanimously blocked the Republican-drawn congressional map on 3 September, restoring the pre-2025 6-2 districts for November and sending redistricting to a statewide referendum. Attorney General Catherine Hanaway said she will appeal immediately to the US Supreme Court, arguing no court has ever overturned a congressional map between a primary and a general election.

Washington Post (left, T1) · Reuters (center, T1) · New York Times (left, T1) · Politico (center, T2) · Al Jazeera (center, T2) · verified, reliable secondary

2 · Trump asks the Supreme Court to let the Postal Service restrict ballots

The Justice Department asked the Supreme Court on 3 September to lift a lower court's block on a Trump rule restricting Postal Service delivery of mail ballots. At least 24 states have sued to stop it, two months before midterms that decide control of Congress.

SCOTUSblog (center, T1) · Associated Press (center wire, T1) · NPR (left, T1) · CBS News (center, T1) · ABC News (center, T1) · verified, reliable secondary

3 · Norway seizes Russian state ship at Svalbard over Crimea award

Norway seized the Russian state research vessel Professor Molchanov at Barentsburg on 2 September under a court order dated 31 August, enforcing Naftogaz's $4.22 billion Hague arbitration award over gas assets expropriated after the annexation of Crimea. Moscow called it piracy, summoned Norway's ambassador and says it will appeal.
