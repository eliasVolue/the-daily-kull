Top line

US strikes sank five Iranian tankers, Iran hit ten ships and a Jordanian air base, Brent cleared $100 for the first time since July, and a Fed hike is now priced.

1 · The World

1 · US sinks five Iranian tankers; Iran hits ten ships and a Jordanian base

Central Command destroyed five Iranian crude tankers, four in the Gulf of Oman and one near Kharg Island, crews ordered off first. The Revolutionary Guard then attacked about ten ships and fired twenty ballistic missiles at Jordan's Muwaffaq Salti air base, eighteen intercepted; one seafarer was killed. Brent traded near $101 late in the European afternoon, its first crossing of $100 since July. Only six ships crossed Hormuz with transponders on in the preceding twenty-four hours.

NBC News (left, T1); Reuters (center, T1); Wall Street Journal (right, T1); NPR (left, T1); Bloomberg (center, T1); ABC News Australia (center, T1); Al Jazeera (center-left, T2); Washington Times (right, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. No Central Command release could be fetched directly, so the strike count rests on multiple T1 outlets across all three lean groups rather than on a primary text. The $101.11 is an intraday quote read at about 16:30 Wednesday, not a settlement: ICE Brent settles at 19:30 London, after this issue's cutoff. A separately reported $102.05 is an outlier and is not used, and a desk attributing the day's move to Houthi strikes on Saudi installations rather than the tanker exchange was overruled, since three desks and the T1 majority tie it to the tanker war. Corroborated after the cutoff: Brent was still above $100 on Thursday morning.

2 · Washington bars Canadian alcohol, dairy and vehicles from importation

The White House signed five proclamations on Tuesday excluding certain Canadian alcoholic beverages, dairy products and motor vehicle products from importation into the United States, and modifying the scope of the additional duties already imposed. The stated ground is continued discrimination against the commerce of the United States, the language of Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act. Canada's C$27.6 billion counter-tariffs took effect Tuesday.

White House presidential actions, 8 September, five proclamations read by title (primary, T1); CBC (center, T1); CNBC (center, T1); Wall Street Journal (right, T1); CNN (left, T1); ABC News (center, T1). Verification: verified, primary source for the proclamations and their date. Narrowed from the draft: the proclamation titles support exclusions on alcohol, dairy and motor vehicle products and a modified duty scope, and nothing more. A 29 September start date, fifty percent tariffs on aluminium, cheese, furniture, mattresses and motorboats, and the stripping of Canadian goods from over $50 billion of federal procurement are carried by secondary reporting only and are not stated as verified here.

3 · Israel shuts Britain's consulate over the settlement trade move

Israel ordered Britain's East Jerusalem consulate closed within thirty days, barred British politicians from entry and ended its training of Palestinian Authority security forces. It is the retaliation for the twelve-state move against settlement goods, of which Britain was the only signatory to impose an actual ban. Washington declined to join the twelve.

CNN (left, T1); NPR (left, T1); Times of Israel (right, T2); Al Jazeera (center-left, T2); Al-Monitor (center, T2); Euronews (center, T2); Washington Times (right, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The twelve-state statement itself shipped Tuesday against the primary joint text, so only Israel's response is new here. Two claims were cut for want of support: the exact number of British politicians barred, and a report that France and Canada have moved from pledged intent to legislation.

2 · By region

Middle East · Egypt, Greece and Cyprus route Cypriot gas into Europe

The El Alamein declaration of 8 September commits the three governments to linking Cyprus's Cronos field to Egypt's Zohr and Damietta plants as soon as possible, 2.8 million tonnes of liquefied natural gas a year from 2028, alongside a 3,000 megawatt Egypt to Greece electricity cable.

Euronews Business carrying Reuters (center, T1 originator); Cyprus Mail (center, T2); Wood Mackenzie (analyst, T2); Daily News Egypt (center, T3). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The declaration text itself was not fetched; the tonnage and the cable rating come from carriers reading it.

Asia and Pacific · China's exports jump 25 percent, surplus to $119.1 billion

August exports rose 25 percent year on year, semiconductors 129.8 percent and cars 43 percent, lifting the monthly trade surplus to $119.1 billion from $112.5 billion in July. Trade is being redirected by the tariff war rather than reduced.

China customs (primary, T1, via carriers); Bloomberg (center, T1); Associated Press via the Washington Post (left, T1); South China Morning Post (center, T2); CNBC (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The customs release is dated Tuesday, so this is inside the window only at its edge and reaches you here for the first time. A "record monthly surplus" superlative was cut: it is carried by one outlet and could not be checked against the full monthly series.

Europe · Hungary again blocks Ukraine's accession clusters

Hungarian diplomats declined to open negotiating clusters two and three, internal market and competition, tying progress to Ukraine legislating the June Transcarpathia minority-rights deal. Budapest lifted its blanket veto in June but keeps unanimity as leverage.

Euronews (center, T2); Ukrainska Pravda (center, T2); The Parliament Magazine (center, T3); Hungarian Conservative (right, T3). Verification: single source, unconfirmed. No T1 source and no primary Council text; the outlets are not clearly independent of one another. A claim that this is the third such block was cut as unsupported.

3 · Energy

1 · Dutch gas hits €79.78, up about a third in a month

TTF front month reached €79.78 per megawatt hour, up 5.2 percent on the day and roughly a third on the month, the highest in more than three years. European Union storage stood at 67.1 percent on the 8 September gas day. Goldman Sachs argues December TTF must clear €100 to pull enough liquefied natural gas to Europe.

Trading Economics (center, T2); Euronews (center, T2); Bloomberg (center, T1); Gas Infrastructure Europe AGSI+ via carriers (primary data, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Independently corroborated by a direct read on Thursday morning showing €78.80 and a monthly gain of 34 percent, so the Wednesday level and the trend both stand. The superlative's vintage differs by carrier, December 2022 against early 2023, so the conservative "more than three years" is used. No storage mandate figure appears here: carriers give both 90 percent by 1 November and 80 percent by 1 December, and the conflict is unresolved.

2 · Ringhals 4 slips to 30 November; half the Swedish nuclear fleet is down

Vattenfall pushed Ringhals 4's restart to 30 November, over a month late, on unforeseen maintenance. With Ringhals 3 and Forsmark 1 also offline, 3,311 of 7,054 megawatts is unavailable. SE3 day-ahead averaged €102.98 on Wednesday against €44.84 on Tuesday, and SE4 hit a quarter-hour peak of €200.04.

Nordisk Kärnkraft live reactor status, giving Ringhals 4 return 30 November, Ringhals 3 31 October and Forsmark 1 17 November (primary-equivalent, T1); energy-charts.info reading ENTSO-E and Nord Pool day-ahead, SE1 €79.59, SE2 €78.72, SE4 €119.69, SE4 quarter-hour peak €200.04 at 08:15 (primary-equivalent, T1); euenergy.live (center, T3); Sweden Herald (center, T3). Verification: single source, unconfirmed for the 30 November announcement itself, which rests on one T3 outlet plus a live reactor register; Vattenfall's own newsroom carries no September release that could be reached. The price levels are the solid half. The reported day-on-day percentage jumps for SE1 and SE2 could not be recomputed from the raw series and are not quoted.

3 · IEA tells governments to hold strategic gas, not just fill targets

The International Energy Agency published new gas security work on Wednesday under the heading that governments have opportunities to strengthen natural gas security after a series of supply shocks, arguing for strategic gas held outside commercial markets rather than reliance on rigid storage fill targets.

International Energy Agency, 9 September (primary, T1, headline and date confirmed on the agency's own site); Euronews (center, T2); Mirage News (center, T3). Verification: verified, primary source for the publication and its date. Two things were cut: a report title that the agency's own listing does not carry, and the figures of 10 to 15 billion cubic metres stored in post-war Ukraine plus pooled LNG call options, which come from a carrier reading a document whose text would not render.

4 · Economy

1 · Hundred dollar oil makes a September Fed hike the base case

CME FedWatch put the chance of a 25 basis point increase on 16 September near 59 percent, from about 49 percent a week earlier. The ten-year Treasury traded near 4.81 percent Wednesday, its highest since October 2023, and Treasury enlarged its long-end buyback operations. August consumer prices land Friday.

CME FedWatch via CNBC (center, T1); Trading Economics (center, T2); US Treasury (primary, T1); Bureau of Labor Statistics release calendar (primary, T1). Verification: single source, unconfirmed for the 59 percent, which traces to one carrier reading the FedWatch tool and could not be fetched directly. The yield is a market quote, not an official print: the Federal Reserve's own H.15 series had posted only 4.78, for 4 September, at the time of writing, and this desk has twice been burned by using a market quote as the table value. The buyback size is left unstated because two sources conflict, one giving at least $4 billion per operation against a usual $2 billion and another up to $6 billion.

2 · China's factory gate inflation accelerates to 3.8 percent

August producer prices rose 3.8 percent year on year and 0.4 percent on the month; consumer prices rose 0.8 percent. The statistics bureau attributes the producer move to imported crude and nonferrous metal prices. The world's largest goods exporter is now passing the energy shock outward rather than supplying the cheap goods that held European inflation down.

National Bureau of Statistics of China, release list of 9 September, both headline figures read directly from the bureau's own postings (primary, T1); CNBC (center, T1); ING Think (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. Both numbers were confirmed independently twice against the bureau, once by the data desk and once at the fact-check. A "highest in three months" superlative and a 3.6 percent consensus were dropped as secondary and unchecked.

3 · The Bund closes at a fifteen-year high, a day before the ECB

Germany's ten-year Bund closed near 3.45 percent Wednesday, up about nine basis points on the day and its highest since April 2011. It caps a euro area long-end selloff of five to nine basis points across France, Belgium, Italy, Denmark, Poland, the Netherlands and Greece. European equities fell, with the STOXX 600 down 0.7 percent.

Trading Economics dated 9 September (center, T2), cross-read against this newsroom's own data desk, which measured the same selloff in progress across two independent providers; Reuters via Investing.com (center, T1); Euronews (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. This corrects the draft, which had the Bund near 3.38 percent and the superlative at August 2011; the completed Wednesday close is about 3.45 and the record reaches back to April 2011. The expectation that the ECB raises its deposit rate to 2.50 percent on Thursday is single source, unconfirmed: the rate pages consulted contradict each other on the direction of the next move, so no ECB expectation is stated as fact here.

5 · Politics

1 · Sweden's Liberals clear the threshold four days before the vote

Novus published a poll on Wednesday putting the Liberals at 5.8 percent, up from the 4.3 percent in its own poll published 7 September, and the first reading this term it calls statistically clear of the four percent threshold. The opposition bloc stands at 50.7 percent against the Tidö parties' 48.1. DN/Ipsos had the Liberals at 3.5 percent on Monday.

Novus own release, read directly on the pollster's site (primary, T1); TV4 (center, T2); SVT (center, T1); Reuters (center, T1); Dagens Nyheter reporting DN/Ipsos (center-left, T2). Verification: verified, primary source for the 5.8 percent and its 9 September date. The comparison to 4.3 is this newsroom's own verified record from Tuesday's issue. Field dates of 6 to 8 September, a sample of 2,292 and the TV4 commission are not confirmed on the primary release and are carried by the broadcaster. A claim that the Liberals rose from 1.9 percent on 19 August was dropped: nothing supports it, and it conflicts with the pollster's own series. Two pollsters still disagree by more than two points on the number that decides the election.

2 · Saxony-Anhalt closes on a non-party premier to block the AfD

Green co-leader Banaszak and the Social Democrats' Alexander Schweitzer backed the plan for a cross-party minister-president from no party. The AfD's deputy made Ulrich Siegmund's election a precondition, and Siegmund refused to be elected on abstentions. The Landtag must constitute itself by 6 October, and the alternative is Germany's first far-right state government since 1945.

ZDF (center, T1); dpa via GMX (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The plan itself shipped Tuesday; what is new is the Green and Social Democrat backing and the AfD's response. The 1945 claim is about governing, not winning: the AfD took the largest share in Thuringia on 1 September 2024 and did not form a government, a distinction this desk got wrong once already.

3 · Kavanaugh refuses Missouri's map; a district judge orders it used

Justice Kavanaugh turned down Missouri's request to use a congressional map expected to aid Republicans, leaving the existing map in place. A federal district judge is reported to have ordered the Republican map used the same week, and state officials say they will follow it. Ballots are due 19 September.

SCOTUSblog, 8 September (specialist primary, T1); Washington Post (left, T1); NBC News (center, T1); JURIST (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary for the Kavanaugh order, which is dated Tuesday 8 September, not Wednesday as drafted, and which SCOTUSblog carries without the case name the draft used. The conflicting district court order and the 19 September ballot deadline are single source, unconfirmed; the two rulings could not be confirmed as landing on the same day.
