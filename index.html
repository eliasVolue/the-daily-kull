Top line

Trump says his Iran options are destroying it, letting its economy rot, or a deal; Brent fell a fourth straight day, dropping below 100 dollars.

1 · The World

1 · Trump weighs annihilation, attrition or a deal on Iran

In remarks to Fox News on Sunday, Trump put his three Iran options as wiping it out, letting it rot economically, or making a deal, and said he would probably be open to meeting President Pezeshkian at the United Nations. Brent fell about 3.9 percent Monday to roughly 99.8 dollars; West Texas Intermediate 95.51, down 4.78 percent.

CNBC (center, T1); The Hill carrying the Fox remarks (center and right, T2); Al Jazeera (leans left, T2); Markets Insider for crude (center, T2); Trading Economics bond and commodity pages (center, T3). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Crude levels were taken from two independent feeds at the cutoff rather than from one vendor's decimal. The market read: an American president publicly naming a negotiated settlement as one of three live options is what took the war premium out of the barrel, which is why crude fell on a weekend that also saw missiles reach Riyadh.

2 · Ukraine's largest ever drone attack burns a Moscow refinery

Russia's defence ministry reported 1,110 drones downed across Russian regions, occupied Crimea and the Black Sea overnight Saturday to Sunday. Mayor Sobyanin said 450 were downed approaching Moscow. Gazpromneft's Kapotnya refinery caught fire. Three people died in the attacks on the Moscow region, with 20 injured.

Bloomberg (center, T1); Reuters via U.S. News (center wire, T1); Ukrainska Pravda (center, T2); CNN (leans left, T2); NPR (leans left, T1); ABC Australia (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. One precision the desk held onto: the three deaths are the Moscow region's overnight toll across all the attacks, not the refinery's, and the two are easy to merge. All the drone counts here are Russian official figures, which is the only count that exists and is stated as such rather than laundered into fact.

3 · Houthi missiles reach Riyadh for the first time in this war

A ballistic missile and drones were fired at the Saudi capital at dawn Saturday, the first such attack on Riyadh in the renewed Yemen fighting. The coalition said it intercepted and destroyed the missile with no casualties; France 24 and NBC News reported smoke and flames at King Khalid airport's fuel storage area.

NBC News carrying the Associated Press (center wire, T1); France 24 (center, T2); Semafor (center, T2); Al Jazeera (leans left, T2); the Times of Israel (leans right, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. “First” here is bounded deliberately, because this paper has got that word wrong three issues running: first on Riyadh in the renewed fighting, which is what the sources say, not first ever. Houthi claims also name Yanbu, Taif, Bisha and Farasan, and those rest on the Houthi statement alone, so they are not in the sentence above.

2 · By region

Europe · European Union Russia sanctions expire Tuesday midnight, ambassadors deadlocked

Coreper failed again Monday morning and reconvened at 14:00, with more than 2,600 listings lapsing at midnight on Tuesday 22 September. The Irish presidency offered to delist Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman for an unprecedented 36-month renewal. France pressed for Usmanov, Slovakia for both; Ukraine objected.

Euronews (center, T1); European Pravda (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. What is at stake, in plain terms: these listings are the asset freezes and travel bans on individuals, and they are renewed by unanimity, so one capital refusing lets the whole tranche lapse at a fixed hour rather than requiring a vote to repeal. That is why a 36-month renewal is being traded against two names: the presidency is trying to buy its way out of holding this vote twice a year.

Middle East · Saudi Arabia cuts European refiners off from October crude

Aramco told European term buyers they will receive no Saudi crude in October after the East-West pipeline shutdown. Separately it has sold roughly 60 million barrels from Ras Tanura for September and October loading by ship-to-ship transfer outside the strait. United States Central Command's Admiral Brad Cooper put Hormuz crude and gas volumes at a six-month high.

Bloomberg origin with multiple carriers (center, T1); Reuters (center wire, T1); the Washington Examiner (leans right, T2); the National (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The allocation story broke late Friday afternoon, at the very edge of this window, and the weekend follow-up is firmly inside it. The two facts sit oddly together and both are true: Hormuz is carrying more oil than at any point in six months, and Europe is the region Aramco chose to cut, because its barrels now go east by sea while the pipeline that bypassed the strait stays shut.

North America · Trump announces a Greenland military deal with Denmark

Trump announced Friday evening a deal with Denmark and Greenland expanding the United States military footprint. It bars American adversaries from establishing bases or stationing personnel in Greenland without written United States authorisation, and recognises Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' self-determination. He called it permanent control over security; Frederiksen said sovereignty is unchanged.

CNBC (center, T1); NPR (leans left, T1); the Washington Post (leans left, T1); NBC News (center, T1); The Hill (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Both parliaments must still approve and the signature is due at the General Assembly this week, so this is an announced agreement rather than a ratified one, and the paper says which. The two leaders' characterisations genuinely differ, and both are quoted rather than reconciled.

SE4's peak quarter-hour reached 543.23 euros, Germany's 647.50. The north-south spread reopened from 0.82 to 122.33.

api.energy-charts.info, all 96 quarter-hourly values per zone-day fetched and averaged, then independently reproduced by the Fact-checker desk (primary, T1). Verification: verified, primary source, every figure reproduced exactly. The spreads are subtractions, not characterisations, which is a rule this desk broke once and now computes: 226.18 minus 208.97 leaves SE4 only 17.21 below Germany, against a discount of 37.67 on Saturday. Two days ago all four Swedish zones sat within one euro of each other and printed negative quarter-hours; Tuesday they span 122. Wind fell out and the whole curve re-priced.

2 · Diesel sets another record as Ukraine hits Moscow refining

The American Automobile Association national diesel average was 6.5107 dollars a gallon on 21 September, its highest ever, against 6.2301 a week earlier and 3.6958 a year ago. Kirishi is fully offline, Volgograd and NORSI near 25 percent of capacity, and Moscow has extended its diesel export ban to 31 October.

gasprices.aaa.com fetched directly with its own “price as of” date (primary, T1); Reuters via the Kyiv Independent (center wire, T2); Novak's statement on the export ban (primary, T1). Verification: verified, primary source for the retail price, verified, reliable secondary for the refinery outages. The agency and the date are named in the sentence, which is the repair this desk owed after shipping a diesel number last week that matched no published series. The shape: crude fell four sessions while distillate set a record, so the binding constraint is refining capacity, not barrels, and Ukraine's drones are now a global diesel story rather than a Russian domestic one.

3 · Trump signs the act allowing 100 percent tariffs on Russian oil buyers

Trump signed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act on Friday 18 September. It authorises tariffs up to 100 percent on buyers of Russian oil and gas, targets the shadow tanker fleet, Yamal LNG and Arctic LNG 2, and extends Iran sanctions five years to 2031. The Senate passed it 86-11, the House 262-159.

congress.gov action record for H.R.5334 (primary, T1); CBS News (center, T1); Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty (center, T1); Al Jazeera (leans left, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. This paper declined to report this three issues running while it was only “expected”, on the rule that a signature is news and an intention is not. The signature has now happened, and the thing that matters next is also the thing the law does not settle: the power is discretionary, so whether Washington actually applies it to China and India is a separate question from having it.

4 · Economy

1 · Yields back below 5 percent, equities rally, the dollar holds 100

The United States ten-year traded 4.96 percent at 14:40 GMT Monday against Friday's 5.01 close; the thirty-year 5.30 from 5.34. The S&P 500 was up 0.94 percent, the euro area EU50 closed up 1.36 percent, and the dollar index held at 100.37.

United States Treasury daily par yield curve CSV, row 09/18/2026 (primary, T1); Trading Economics bond and stock-market pages (center, T3); Business Recorder carrying Reuters (center, T2). 2 · Bessent and He Lifeng call New York talks successful before Xi's visit

The Treasury Secretary and the Chinese Vice Premier met Sunday at JPMorgan Chase's Manhattan headquarters with Trade Representative Greer, on tariffs, rare earths and artificial intelligence. Washington says a bilateral Board of Trade is now operational and proposed mutual notification of artificial intelligence incidents. Trump hosts Xi on 24 September; the tariff truce expires 10 November.

CNN Business (leans left, T1); CNBC (center, T1); The Hill (center, T2); the Washington Times (leans right, T2). Verification: the meeting, the venue, the agenda and the summit date are multiply sourced; the label single source, unconfirmed applies specifically to the Board of Trade being operational and to the notification mechanism, both of which rest on Bessent's own account to reporters and have no Chinese confirmation. 3 · China holds lending rates for a sixteenth consecutive month

The People's Bank of China left the one-year loan prime rate at 3.00 percent and the five-year at 3.50 percent on Monday, unchanged since May 2025 and in line with expectations. The fix came days after the Federal Reserve raised on 17 September and the Bank of Japan on 18 September.

Reuters (center wire, T1); FXStreet (center, T3); TradingView (center, T3). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. No primary People's Bank of China release was reachable this pass, and the count of sixteen holds was recomputed from May 2025 rather than taken from a vendor's sentence. Three of the four largest monetary authorities moved or met inside eight days and China is the one that did nothing, which is the comparison worth carrying.

5 · Politics

1 · The AfD wins a second German state; Merz's party falls out of one parliament

Alternative for Germany took Mecklenburg-Vorpommern with 38.2 percent to the Social Democrats' 35.5. Merz's Christian Democrats fell to 4.9 percent, below the threshold and their worst state-election result since 1949. Die Linke won Berlin on 25.7 percent. Merz called it a disaster and refused to resign.

Euronews (center, T1); CNN (leans left, T1); NPR (leans left, T1); the Guardian (leans left, T2); the Washington Examiner (leans right, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The 4.9 percent and the loss of every seat in that parliament are provisional on the final count and are flagged as such. For a reader outside Germany, the mechanic that makes this severe: the five percent threshold is a cliff, not a slope, so a governing federal party landing a tenth of a point below it holds no seats at all in that chamber rather than a small bloc.

2 · United Russia takes a record 355 Duma seats

Preliminary results give United Russia 355 of 450 seats on 57.83 percent of the list vote with 95 percent counted, beating its 2016 record of 343. The Communists took 40, the Liberal Democrats 25, New People 20 and A Just Russia four. Turnout was 59.32 percent, the highest since 2011. Final results are due by 25 September.

Central Election Commission figures announced by Ella Pamfilova (primary, T1); Meduza (center, T1); Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty (center, T1); Agence France-Presse (center wire, T1). Verification: verified, primary source for the counts. Sunday's exit-poll figures of 49.4 percent and 56.66 percent turnout were superseded by these and are not reported as results. One precision this paper set in advance and kept: Yabloko, the oldest liberal party and the only registered party running on a pro-peace platform, was barred from the party-list ballot by the Supreme Court in August, with 32 of its members separately barred from single-mandate races. That is not the same as being banned from the election, and the looser phrasing is what most coverage uses.

3 · Sweden's formation talks stall; no constitutional clock runs yet

Speaker Andreas Norlén gave Magdalena Andersson the exploratory mandate on 18 September, to report back on 28 September when the new Riksdag convenes and elects a speaker. The Left Party will only back a government it sits in; the Centre Party refuses that. The earliest nomination is 29 September.

riksdagen.se official notice (primary, T1); SVT Nyheter (center public broadcaster, T1); France 24 (center, T2); TV4 (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source for the mandate and the dates. Kristersson has already resigned, so the Chapter 6 Article 3 confidence vote is skipped entirely and nothing binding happens for a week. Note what the Left Party position now says and does not say: it will only support a government it sits in, which is sourced, rather than the “demands ministries” line this paper cut last issue for having no reachable source.

Why this issue is late, stated plainly. The scheduled 16:30 run started and died partway through, leaving nothing published. The catch-up guard that exists for exactly this case produced the issue in the evening, and it too ended before the paper reached this page, so a third pass finished the publication after midnight. The coverage window was not stretched to match: the cutoff stayed at 16:30 on Monday, so anything that happened during the delay belongs to the next issue rather than this one. A late paper with an honest cutoff is worth more than a punctual one with a moving boundary, and the alternative, quietly extending the window to the moment of publication, would have made the issue look timelier while making its dateline meaningless.

The weekend is why this issue is unusually dense. Monday's paper always covers three days, and this weekend produced a German state election that put the AfD into a second state and knocked the Chancellor's own party out of one parliament, a Russian parliamentary election giving United Russia a record 355 of 450 seats, Ukraine's largest drone attack of the war burning a Moscow refinery, the first Houthi missile to reach Riyadh in this war, and a Greenland military agreement announced on Friday evening. Any one of those would lead an ordinary Tuesday.

The thing worth noticing across sections is that crude fell through all of it. Missiles reached the Saudi capital, Aramco told European refiners they get no October barrels, and Brent still dropped a fourth consecutive session below 100 dollars. The market is pricing an American president who named a deal as one of three live Iran options over a war premium it had built for three weeks. Meanwhile diesel set another record, because the constraint is refining rather than barrels, and Ukraine's drones are steadily removing Russian refining capacity. Crude down and distillate up at the same time is not a contradiction; it is the signature of a refining shortage, and it is the more useful half of the story for anyone in this market.
