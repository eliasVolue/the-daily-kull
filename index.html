Top line

Russia answered the Kerch strikes with over 500 missiles and drones, hitting three Kyiv bridges and targeting a key western grid node, as an AI sell-off spread to Asia.

1 · The World

1 · Russia's mass strike hits Kyiv bridges, targets western grid

Overnight into 20 November Russia fired 84 cruise missiles, 27 ballistic missiles and 404 drones at Ukraine, killing at least eight. Three Kyiv bridges were hit and the Zakhidnoukrainska 750 kV substation, a key western grid node, was targeted, after Putin vowed “far more painful” strikes.

Reuters (center, T2, eight killed); Kyiv Independent (center, T2, nine killed); BBC, Financial Times and Politico (center, T2, bridges); Meduza (center, T2); Nasha Niva (center, T2, cruise missile count); Kyiv Post (center, T2, Putin's vow). The ballistic missile and drone counts could not be independently confirmed; damage to the substation was not disclosed. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · AI sell-off spreads from Wall Street to Asia and Europe

The S&P 500 fell 2.02 percent on 19 November to 7,175.83, a sixth straight loss, leaving it 8.2 percent below its record of last month. On 20 November the Nikkei closed down 5.06 percent, the Kospi 5.2 percent and SoftBank 14.5 percent; European tech stocks tumbled.

AP (center, T2); Jiji Press and CNBC (center, T2); The Wall Street Journal (right, T2, European tech, 09:02 GMT 20 November); Reuters via The Star (center, T2). Friday's US close came after the cutoff and is left for Monday. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · Houthis threaten Jeddah and all Saudi Red Sea ports

On 20 November the Saudi-led coalition struck Yemen's Hodeidah port after warning civilians to leave. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree replied that Jeddah and all Saudi Red Sea ports are now targets and told ships to leave. Red Sea ports such as Yanbu carry the Saudi oil that bypasses Hormuz.

Al Jazeera with Reuters and AFP (center, T2); The National (center, T2); France 24 (center, T2); Maritime Executive (T2). The point about Yanbu is this paper's context, not the sources'. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · By region

Europe · Estonia shuts its Russian road border for six months

On 20 November Estonia's government approved closing all five road crossings with Russia, Narva included, from 1 December for an initial six months, to be reviewed. Moscow called it a new “Iron Curtain”. Officials said it was not tied to the latest sabotage attempt.

ERR, Estonia's public broadcaster (center, T2); RTÉ (center, T2); Al Jazeera (center, T2); The Moscow Times (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

North America · Senate rejects limit on Trump's Iran war, 51 to 48

On 19 November the Senate defeated a war powers resolution the House passed in September; Republicans Collins, Murkowski and Paul backed it. A Pentagon official put the war's cost at 48.3 billion dollars. The Senate declined to restrain a war that drives oil and gas prices.

AP via Anchorage Daily News (center, T2); CBS News and ABC News (center, T2); Stars and Stripes (center, T2); Democracy Now (left, T2). The resolution would not have forced an end to the war. The time of the vote was not published; a vote before the window opened is unlikely but not ruled out. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

Africa · Ebola: 11,613 confirmed cases in Congo

WHO's update of 20 November counts 11,613 confirmed cases and 5,678 deaths as of 17 November, 945 more cases since 6 November, with transmission in 48 of 68 health zones. It remains a global health emergency.

WHO Disease Outbreak News, 20 November (T1); Al Jazeera (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source.

3 · Energy

1 · Brent heads for a third weekly loss near 91 dollars

At 16:00 CET on 20 November Brent traded at 91.31 dollars a barrel, down 67 cents and 4 to 5 percent lower on the week, as Gulf crude shipments recover despite near daily tanker attacks. OPEC+ meets 29 November to set 2027 output.

Reuters via RTÉ and CNBC (center, T2); Bloomberg via Rigzone (center, T2); Al-Monitor (center, T2); UOB (T2, attack frequency). Reuters says shipments are back near pre-war levels, an unattributed line that conflicts with transit counts; The Wall Street Journal (right) reports the UAE running risky Hormuz operations. Friday's settlement came after the cutoff. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · US Senate passes grid permitting overhaul, 62 to 33

Late on 19 November US time the Senate passed a permitting bill that widens the federal grid regulator's power over interstate transmission, makes data centres pay for grid upgrades and limits environmental review lawsuits. Its fate in the House is uncertain.

CBS News, NBC News and Axios (center, T2); Deseret News (T2); Latitude Media and ENR (T2, bill details). The 230 kV threshold reported by one desk could not be confirmed and is left out. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · SE3 day-ahead falls to 93 euros for Saturday

Friday's auction for 21 November delivery cleared SE3 at 93.32 euros per MWh, down from 137.51; SE4 at 142.98 from 204.65 and Germany at 187.88 from 214.51. NO1 cleared at 179.20, nearly double SE3, as weekend demand fell.

energy-charts.info day-ahead, daily average of all 96 quarter-hours, recomputed by the desk and again by the Fact-checker (T1). Verification: verified, primary source.

4 · Economy

1 · Anthropic opens 34 percent up, closes 17 percent up

Anthropic first traded on Nasdaq at 17:50 CET on 19 November at 111.50 dollars, 34 percent above its 83 dollar offer price, and closed at 97.17, up 17 percent, after raising a record 81.3 billion dollars. Disclosure: this paper is produced with Claude, an Anthropic model.

NPR (left, T2); AP via ITV (center, T2); Al Jazeera (center, T2); Fox Business (right, T2, first trade 11:52 New York time); Irish Times (center, T2). Friday's trading came after the cutoff. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · Investors buy Treasuries despite a near certain Fed hike

The ten year Treasury yield ended 19 November at 5.04 percent, down from 5.07, and fell further in Friday trading as investors sought safety from the stock rout. CNBC put futures odds of a rate rise on 9 December at about 92 percent.

US Treasury daily par yield curve (T1); CNBC, 05:21 New York time 20 November (center, T2); Reuters via Yahoo Finance (center, T2). The yields are primary; the 92 percent figure was found only at CNBC. Friday's close came after the cutoff. Verification: verified, primary source (yields); single source, unconfirmed (hike odds).

3 · Japan's inflation rises less than expected, to 2.3 percent

Japan's October consumer prices, released 20 November, rose 2.3 percent on a year earlier, up from 2.1 but below a 2.5 forecast; excluding fresh food and energy, inflation slowed to 2.2 from 2.4. Above the 2 percent target, it keeps a Bank of Japan rise from 1.25 percent on 17 to 18 December in play.

Statistics Bureau of Japan (T1, read directly by the data desk); Japan Times (center, T2); investinglive (T3); Reuters poll (center, T2, 86 percent of economists expect a December rise). Verification: verified, primary source.

5 · Politics

1 · UK and EU agree to link their carbon markets

At their Brussels summit on 20 November Prime Minister Andy Burnham and EU leaders agreed to link their emissions trading systems from 1 January 2028, with mutual exemption from carbon border levies, plus a food standards deal and a youth scheme. The EU ties it to a UK tuition fee deal.

UK government joint statement and release, 14:38 GMT (T1); Financial Times (center, T2); Al Jazeera with Reuters (center, T2); AP (center, T2); RTÉ and Irish Times (center, T2). Electricity market talks are due to conclude in 2027. Verification: verified, primary source.

2 · Moscow invites Britain, Germany and France to talks

On 20 November deputy foreign minister Sergei Ryabkov said Moscow had invited the three to exploratory Ukraine talks, with no guarantee of a later seat. Germany's Pistorius called it positive; Paris demands full participation.

Brussels Times with Belga, citing Interfax (center, T2); TASS (state media, Ryabkov's words only); Daily Finland (T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · COP31 heads into overtime with fossil fuels deleted

COP31 in Antalya was heading past its scheduled Friday close with no deal. The latest draft, issued 19 November, drops all fossil fuel references and the 120 billion dollar adaptation target, stepping back from the Dubai pledge to transition away from fossil fuels.

Reuters (center, T2, 18:43 GMT 19 November); The Independent (left, T2); UN News (T1). What happened after the cutoff is left for Monday's issue. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.
