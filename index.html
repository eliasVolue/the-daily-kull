Top line

Trump, Xi and Putin adopted a Shenzhen declaration promising more fuel supply but silent on Ukraine and Iran, as Putin ruled out an energy ceasefire after Ukraine's third Kerch strike.

1 · The World

1 · Shenzhen summit ends in a declaration, no breakthroughs

Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen on 19 November and adopted a four point declaration: nuclear risk talks, UN reform, more refined fuel supply, curbs on the International Criminal Court. Ukraine and Iran go unnamed, and no extension of the US-China trade truce, which expires 10 January, was announced.

Al Jazeera (center, T2); declaration text via Anadolu, The National and Xinhua (center, T2); NBC News and CBS News (center, T2); Euronews and France 24 (center, T2); Nikkei Asia (center, T2); AP (center, T2, headline); The Washington Post (left, T2, headline); The Wall Street Journal and The Times (right, T2, headlines). Washington had not published its own version of the text by the cutoff. The declaration pledges more fuel supply but gives no volumes or dates. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · Third Kerch Bridge strike; Putin rules out an energy ceasefire

Ukraine hit the Crimean Bridge for the third time in four days overnight into 19 November, halting rail traffic. Putin vowed a “much more painful” reply and said Russia would not negotiate a maritime or energy ceasefire, keeping refinery and grid strikes in play.

The Moscow Times (center, T2); Kyiv Post (center, T2); ABC News and CBS News (center, T2); Euronews (center, T2); Ukrainska Pravda (center, T2); BBC and The Guardian (center and left, T2, headlines). Ukraine's General Staff claims Neptune missiles; the extent of damage differs by source. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · Trump weighs a renewed Iran air campaign

The New York Times reports Trump is weighing a renewed offensive on Iran; Netanyahu wants a joint four to seven day air campaign, and Israeli plans include strikes on energy infrastructure, an option Trump has not agreed to. A US official says economic pressure makes major combat unnecessary.

The New York Times (left, T2, via The Jerusalem Post and Times of Israel); Axios (center, T2, on the Mossad intelligence behind the push); Stars and Stripes (center, T2). Anonymous officials. Iran's claim that tankers hit mines east of Hormuz is unconfirmed by UKMTO, the British maritime security agency, and left out. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · By region

Middle East · Houthi strike damages Saudi wealth fund tower

Early on 19 November a Houthi strike damaged the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh's financial district; no injuries were reported. It is the district's second hit this month, after the Aramco tower on 8 November.

Reuters via syndication (center, T2); France 24 and AFP (center, T2); Arab News (center, T2, Saudi owned); The National (center, T2); Times of Israel (center, T2); The Wall Street Journal (right, T2, headline); Bloomberg (center, T2, headline). One report says the damage came from debris of an intercepted missile, not a direct hit; how many floors were hit differs by report, so it is left out. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

Asia and Pacific · World Court finds Myanmar committed genocide

The International Court of Justice ruled on 19 November, 9 to 6, that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time it has held a state responsible for genocide, and ordered prosecutions and reparations. It could set the bar for South Africa's case against Israel.

UN News (T1); American Society of International Law (T1, vote and remedies); CNN (left, T2); Al Jazeera (center, T2); SVT (center, T2); The Jerusalem Post (right, T2). In 2007 the court found Serbia had only failed to prevent genocide. Compensation is to be set later. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

Latin America · Hegseth signs a military partnership with Venezuela

In Caracas on 18 November US Defense Secretary Hegseth announced joint training, intelligence sharing and equipment transfers with interim President Delcy Rodríguez, tying the largest proven oil reserves to Washington. The text is unpublished.

Reuters via US News (center, T2, 17:58 GMT, inside the window); AP via Korea Herald (center, T2); Al Jazeera (center, T2); Air and Space Forces Magazine (T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · Energy

1 · IEA outlook: the Gulf war has cost 2.8 billion barrels

The IEA's World Energy Outlook, published 19 November, counts 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 bcm of gas lost from the Gulf since February, sees oil demand plateau near 103 mb/d by 2030, and a 50 percent LNG supply wave.

IEA World Energy Outlook 2026 executive summary (T1); AFP via Times of Israel (center, T2); Oilprice.com (T3). Verification: verified, primary source.

2 · Ringhals 3 restart slips to 20 December

Vattenfall pushed Ringhals 3's return to 20 December after a valve leak inside containment. With Forsmark 1 back since 18 November and Forsmark 3 since 19 November, SE3 day-ahead for Friday fell to 137.51 euros per MWh from 192.99; SE4 204.65, Germany 214.51.

Vattenfall press release and market notice (T1); TT via Privata Affärer (center, T2, gives 21 December); energy-charts.info day-ahead for 20 November delivery, recomputed from all 96 quarter-hours (T1). Ringhals 4 is still due 22 November. Verification: verified, primary source.

3 · EU delays methane rules for fuel imports by two years

EU Parliament and Council provisionally agreed on 19 November to push methane monitoring and reporting duties for oil, gas and coal importers to 1 January 2029, and methane intensity reporting to August 2030, citing supply risks and high prices after a US lobbying campaign.

Reuters via Arab News (center, T2); Bloomberg via Rigzone (center, T2); Eunews (center, T2). It removes a 2027 compliance hurdle for US LNG cargoes to Europe. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

4 · Economy

1 · Fed minutes: almost all officials expect more hikes

Minutes of the October meeting, published 18 November, show almost all Fed officials expect further rate rises, ahead of the 9 December decision. The ten year Treasury yield closed at 5.07 percent on 18 November, the Treasury's own figure.

Federal Reserve minutes (T1); US Treasury par yield curve, 18 November (T1); Bloomberg (center, T2); Reuters via syndication (center, T2). Claims about how many officials would back a bigger hike, and how fully futures price December, could not be checked and are left out. Verification: verified, primary source.

2 · Anthropic prices a record 81.3 billion dollar IPO

Anthropic sold 980 million shares at 83 dollars, above its 70 to 78 range, raising 81.3 billion dollars at about 1.5 trillion fully diluted, the largest IPO by proceeds. Disclosure: this paper is produced with Claude, an Anthropic model.

CNBC (center, T2); BBC (center, T2); Reuters via syndication (center, T2); Washington Times (right, T2); The New York Times (left, T2, headline). The first day of trading falls after this issue's cutoff. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

3 · Thedéen: Riksbank sees gradual rate normalisation

Governor Erik Thedéen told the Riksdag finance committee on 19 November the 2 percent policy rate is “easing off the accelerator” and the bank envisages gradual normalisation; temporary food VAT and fare cuts depress CPIF now. The next rate decision is due in December.

Sveriges Riksbank, hearing presentation and calendar (T1); Börsvärlden (center, T3), the one outlet reporting the hearing's content. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

5 · Politics

1 · Putin sets terms for Europe as Berlin coalition meets

In Shenzhen Putin said Washington is urging Moscow to accept European participation, and invited European envoys to Moscow first. Nearly 40 delegations met in Berlin seeking a seat and binding guarantees; no outcome by the cutoff.

Reuters via US News (center, T2); AFP via Arab News (center, T2); France 24 (center, T2); German Chancellery (T1, nearly 40 delegations); Berliner Zeitung and Tagesspiegel (center left, T2); TASS (state media, Russian quotes only). Verification: verified, reliable secondary.

2 · COP31 draft drops all fossil fuel language

A revised COP31 draft in Antalya on 19 November deleted both references to limiting fossil fuels and the 120 billion dollar adaptation finance target. Talks are due to close Friday; overruns are routine.

Reuters via Japan Times and Globe and Mail (center, T2); UN News (T1); DW (center, T2); CBC (center, T2); Climate Home News (T3). Most reports trace to one Reuters story; UN News is an independent account. Verification: verified, reliable secondary.
