The World

1 · Houthi barrage halts Saudi energy sites; Brent peaks at $99.45

Dozens of Houthi ballistic missiles and drones struck four cities near the Yemeni border on Tuesday, Abha, Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait, hitting Aramco energy facilities as well as an airport and King Khalid Air Base. Saudi Arabia's energy ministry confirmed fires at several locations without naming facilities; Saudi officials said energy sites near Yemen had halted. The coalition's Major General Turki al-Malki said 73 were wounded. Brent touched $99.45 intraday, the highest since 24 July, then eased back near $98 by late afternoon.

2 · Twelve states including Sweden move on settlement trade; Britain bans

Twelve foreign ministries, Sweden's included, confirmed their intention to introduce national restrictions on trade in goods with West Bank settlements, or to support European ones. Only Britain acted today: Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the Commons it will bar settlement goods. The trigger was Israel's E1 tender for 1,234 housing units. US ambassador Mike Huckabee called the move irrational and warned of repercussions; Foreign Minister Gideon Saar said Israel will act against Britain.

3 · Russia breaks the three-day Kyiv pause with its heaviest wave since

The halt on strikes against each other's capitals, ordered by Putin on 5 September and reciprocated by Zelensky for Moscow, ended overnight when Russia struck Kyiv. Ukraine downed or suppressed 142 drones, 31 cruise missiles and two ballistic missiles, 175 targets in all. Three were killed and 25 injured in Kyiv, five killed and 63 injured nationally. The pause was the Witkoff and Kushner shuttle's only concrete output.

By region

Europe · Germany arrests a grid saboteur; the motive is not Russia

Police arrested a 48-year-old man near the Weisweiler plant on Tuesday carrying further explosive devices, over homemade rocket attacks that shorted high-voltage lines in North Rhine-Westphalia and Brandenburg, including devices at the Turnow-Preilack substation feeding 50Hertz. Investigators are treating the attacks as climate-motivated strikes on lignite infrastructure, which would rule out the Russian hybrid-sabotage reading that dominated last week.

North America · Trump threatens a Bombardier ban as tariffs bite

Hours before Canada's C$27.6 billion counter-tariffs took effect at 00:01 Tuesday, Trump said Bombardier cannot sell aircraft in the United States unless it builds there. Bombardier pointed to its US footprint, including about 1,500 jobs in Kansas, whose Wichita hosts its US headquarters. Republican senator Jerry Moran pushed back, the first Republican Senate objection of this trade fight.

Economy

1 · Britain's 30-year gilt sale draws the highest cost since 1998

Britain sold £4.25 billion of 5⅜ percent 2056 gilts by syndication on Tuesday at the highest borrowing cost at any gilt sale since 1998. The Office for Budget Responsibility already projects £109 billion of debt interest this year, 8.4 percent of public spending, before the 28 October budget. Separately, the US ten-year Treasury yield topped 4.8 percent intraday, its highest of the current cycle, before easing near 4.78.

2 · Copper sets a record $14,703 a tonne

London Metal Exchange three-month copper hit a record $14,703 a tonne on Tuesday, up about 1.3 percent and beating Monday's record $14,533. LME stocks are down nearly 40 percent since late May while COMEX warehouses hold a record 766,795 short tons. Storms and guidance cuts in Chile and a US import rush ahead of tariffs are both driving it. Copper is the input cost line under every grid reinforcement and cable order in Europe.

3 · Soft Swedish inflation takes the September Riksbank hike off the table

Sweden's August flash inflation, published Monday, undershot Danske Bank's forecasts across the board: consumer price index 0.28 percent year on year against Danske's 0.59, CPIF 0.72 against 1.00, core CPIF excluding energy 0.52 against 0.74. The policy rate stays 1.75 percent; the full August CPI prints 14 September. Sweden becomes the exception in a tightening world, which is what puts pressure under the euro-krona rate.

1 · Swedish pollsters disagree on whether the Liberals survive

Two polls now bracket the number that decides Sunday's election. DN/Ipsos, published Monday and fielded 25 August to 7 September, puts the Liberals at 3.5 percent and the centre-left bloc at 52.7 against the Tidö parties' 44.9. Novus, fielded 1 to 5 September among 2,888 voters, had the Liberals at 4.3 and the blocs at 50.3 to 47.9. Institutes polling in the last three weeks span 2.3 to 4.3 percent for the Liberals, and a party below four percent has its votes destroyed rather than transferred, worth roughly fifteen seats to the governing bloc.

2 · Anti-AfD parties float a minister-president from no party

BSW proposed an independent minister-president in Saxony-Anhalt leading a cabinet of outside experts, and the Greens said they will talk about it. The arithmetic works: 83 seats, the AfD 39, so 44 elsewhere; a third ballot needs only a simple majority of votes cast, a threshold of 42 with everyone voting, which 44 clears and 39 does not. It is one route to blocking an AfD premier without any party supplying it a vote. The state CDU's leadership collapsed overnight, the executive resigning and Sven Schulze standing down.

3 · Hungary expels ten Russian diplomats

Hungary's foreign minister Anita Orbán said on Tuesday that ten Russian mission staff had engaged in activities unacceptable for diplomats, the country's first expulsion of Russian diplomats since 2018. Maria Zakharova promised a harsh and painful answer and blamed a Russophobic lobby in Budapest. Péter Magyar's Tisza government, in office since May after defeating Orbán's Fidesz in April, has flipped Hungary from the European Union's reliable veto on Russia sanctions to an expeller, and unanimity is the mechanism Sweden's push on frozen Russian assets has to clear.
