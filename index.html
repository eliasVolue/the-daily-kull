The European Central Bank raised rates a second time this cycle, Houthi forces took Mokha beside Bab el-Mandeb, and Brent traded above $105.

1 · The World

1 · Houthis seize Mokha, closing on the Red Sea strait

Houthi forces took the Yemeni port of Mokha on Thursday, under 100 kilometres from Bab el-Mandeb, which carried 4.2 million barrels of oil a day in the first half of 2025. Government troops fell back to Dhubab, on the strait itself. Brent traded as high as $105.84 intraday, after settling at $101.21 on Wednesday.

Reuters (center, T1); Agence France-Presse via Al-Monitor (center, T1); France 24 (center, T1); Euronews (center, T2); The National (center, T2); Times of Israel (right, T2); US Energy Information Administration, World Oil Transit Chokepoints (primary, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The throughput figure is corrected from a desk draft of 5.4 million: the agency's own table gives 4.1 million for 2024 and 4.2 million for the first half of 2025, and no recent year at 5.4. The $105.84 is the day's high on the exchange's historical record, not a settlement, and not the level: Brent was quoted between $104.66 and $105.36 through the European afternoon. ICE Brent settles at 19:30 London, after this issue's cutoff.

2 · The ECB raises rates to 2.50 percent, its second increase of the cycle

Deposit, main refinancing and marginal lending rates each rose 25 basis points, to 2.50, 2.65 and 2.90 percent, effective 16 September. The first increase of this tightening cycle was on 11 June, to 2.25 percent, and July was a pause. Europe is now raising rates into a supply shock rather than a demand boom.

European Central Bank press release ecb.mp260910 (primary, T1); European Central Bank press release ecb.mp260611 for the June increase (primary, T1); CNBC (center, T2); Euronews (center, T2); Morningstar (center, T2); Quartz (left, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. Both desks called this the first hike of the cycle and both were wrong; the Fact-checker found the 11 June release and the claim was corrected here and in the top line before publication.

3 · August tied as the hottest month ever measured

Copernicus reported on Thursday that August averaged 16.96 degrees Celsius, 1.65 above pre-industrial and level with July 2023, the first month above 1.5 since November 2025. Sea surface temperature outside the polar regions set an August record, and western Europe recorded its warmest summer, beating 2003.

Copernicus Climate Change Service monthly bulletin (primary, T1); Scientific American (center-left, T2); Anadolu Agency (center, T2); Euronews (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. One precision applied: the extrapolar sea surface figure of 21.07 degrees is an August record and only the joint highest across all months, level with March 2024, so it is not stated as an outright all-time record.

2 · By region

Europe · NATO caught Russian submersibles rehearsing an Arctic cable attack

British, Norwegian and United States forces intercepted Russian deep-sea submersibles near Svalbard this spring as they rehearsed deploying a weapon built to disable undersea cables untraceably. Two fibre links of 1,400 kilometres each run to the mainland at depths of 2,700 metres, carrying satellite ground-station traffic. No cables were damaged.

Reuters exclusive, carried by The Japan Times, U.S. News, Insurance Journal and MarineLink (center, T1 originator). Verification: single source, unconfirmed. Every carrier reproduces the same Reuters copy. Norway's defence minister is quoted on the record, but that quote was obtained inside the Reuters exclusive, so it is not an independent second source and is not listed as one. A separate Norwegian ministry publication could not be found.

North America · Trump pledges $5,000 to every adult if Republicans hold Congress

At the Republican Party's first midterm convention, in Dallas on Wednesday, Trump promised every adult citizen $5,000, spendable only inside the United States, if Republicans keep both chambers in November. Vance suggested the wealthy would not qualify and pointed to tariff revenue, which was $154.5 billion net through July against a cost estimated above $1 trillion.

CNBC (center, T1); Associated Press via Houston Public Media (center, T1); CNN (left, T1); Fox News (right, T1); Time (left, T2); Treasury and Joint Economic Committee data via USAFacts for the customs receipts (primary, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Vance's exclusion of the wealthy is a suggestion, not an announced threshold, and is written that way. The cost is held at the conservative end: roughly 270 million adults at $5,000 is about $1.35 trillion, and independent estimates run $1.22 to $1.35 trillion.

Asia and Pacific · Nepal asks $5 billion after its hydropower fleet was destroyed

Kathmandu will ask donors for $5 billion for the first phase of recovery, framing the 26 August glacier collapse as climate injustice. More than a dozen hydropower plants, 431 megawatts, are off grid. At least 1,300 are dead and more than 5,300 missing across Nepal and Tibet together.

Reuters exclusive (center, T1); Kathmandu Post (center, T2); Mongabay (center, T2); Nepal Electricity Authority and the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (primary, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The toll is stated with its scope, because Nepal alone is about 1,369 dead and 5,132 missing, with Tibet's 43 and 519 counted separately: a combined figure presented as one country's is an error this desk logged on 2 September. The plant count is the electricity authority's 13 damaged projects, not the eleven a desk drafted. The $5 billion's scope is contested, Reuters framing it as a first phase over five months and the Kathmandu Post as the full reconstruction bill; the narrower reading is used. Reuters filed on 9 September, so this sits at the window's edge.

3 · Energy

1 · Saudi output slumps to its lowest since 1990

Riyadh told OPEC that August crude output fell 1.9 million barrels a day to 6.238 million, below April's wartime low and the least since 1990. Kpler puts August exports at 3.2 million barrels a day, a 13-year low. The producer whose spare capacity has capped every price spike for three decades is now itself a shrinking supplier.

Bloomberg, reading Saudi Arabia's own submission to OPEC (center, T1); Al-Monitor (center-left, T2); Kpler tanker tracking, via Al-Monitor (primary data, T2); Fortune (center, T2). Verification: single source, unconfirmed. The 6.238 million rests on one Bloomberg reading of one submission. A desk's separate line, that exports fell about a third, was cut: it had no stated base, and against the roughly 7 million a day of pre-war exports the fall is about 54 percent. A total OPEC supply figure of 19.91 million was also cut, because it comes from a Bloomberg survey reported 8 September, a different dataset two days older than the Saudi submission.

2 · Dutch gas passes €81, the highest since December 2022

TTF front month traded up to €81.39 intraday on Thursday, up about 2.7 percent on the day and roughly 38 percent on the month. European Union storage stood at 67.1 percent on the 8 September gas day, with only weeks of the injection season left before withdrawals begin.

Trading Economics (center, T3); Il Sole 24 Ore (center, T2); CNBC (center, T1); Euronews (center-left, T2); Gas Infrastructure Europe AGSI+ via carriers for the storage level (primary data, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The day-change arithmetic checks out from two readings of the same page implying the same previous settlement of about €79.25. Two desk claims were cut as unverifiable, that Norwegian flows are below 240 million cubic metres a day and that Algerian maintenance starts 11 September: the nearest match found was a September 2025 note, and seasonal maintenance stories resurface annually in near-identical wording.

3 · The IEA lifts 2026 coal demand to a record on gas-to-coal switching

The agency's Coal Mid-Year Update, published Thursday, raised 2026 global coal demand to a record 8.94 billion tonnes, up 1.2 percent, having previously forecast a slight decline. Liquefied natural gas shipments lost through Hormuz drove switching in Europe, Japan, Korea, China, India and Vietnam.

International Energy Agency news item and Coal Mid-Year Update 2026 (primary, T1); Reuters (center, T1); The Australian (right, T2); BusinessGreen (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. The report title was checked against the agency's own listing, after a title that did not exist was caught on this beat yesterday. This one exists.

4 · Economy

1 · What the ECB decided, and what Lagarde would not say

Staff projections put inflation at 3.0 percent this year, 2.5 next and 2.1 in 2028, with growth revised up to 0.9 percent. Lagarde said the Governing Council did not discuss the path from here and the statement does not pre-commit to one, leaving the market to price the terminal rate. The new rates bind from 16 September, six days after the vote.

European Central Bank press release ecb.mp260910 and the September staff projections (primary, T1); Morningstar (center, T2); FXStreet on the press conference (center, T3); CNBC (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. This resolves an expectation yesterday's issue declined to state as fact, because the rate pages consulted then contradicted each other on the direction of the next move. Declining to guess was the right call and it has now landed on the hawkish side.

2 · US wholesale inflation jumps to 5.4 percent, diesel driving it

Producer prices rose 0.4 percent in August and 5.4 percent over twelve months, against 5.3 percent expected and 4.8 in July. Goods rose 1.1 percent, energy 4.2 and diesel 24.1, which alone was more than a third of the goods increase. The core measure excluding food, energy and trade was 4.7 percent over the year. August consumer prices land Friday.

Bureau of Labor Statistics producer price release, 10 September (primary, T1); Trading Economics for the July rate and the consensus (center, T3); CNBC (center, T2); Washington Examiner (right, T2); Quartz (left, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. A desk line calling the monthly print in line with expectations was cut: no consensus for the monthly figure could be found, and the sourced fact is that the annual rate beat. The 4.7 percent is the twelve-month core change; the monthly core was 0.3 percent.

3 · Treasury's bigger buyback does not stop the long end

Treasury announced on Wednesday a buyback of up to $6 billion held on Thursday. Yields rose anyway: the ten-year traded near 4.92 percent intraday, its highest since October 2023, the Bund near 3.49, its highest since April 2011, and the thirty-year gilt near 5.9, its highest since 1998. That points at inflation expectations rather than a liquidity problem.

US Treasury press release sb0607 of 19 August (primary, T1); Reuters (center, T1); Federal Reserve H.15, latest print 4.80 percent for 8 September (primary, T1); Treasury daily par yield curve, 4.83 percent for 9 September (primary, T1); Trading Economics for the intraday levels (center, T3); CNBC (center, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Every wire called $6 billion triple the normal size. Treasury's own release had raised the standard operation from $2 billion to at least $4 billion with effect from 9 September, so it is half again the new floor, and the wires were measuring against a baseline retired the day before. The yields are intraday market quotes: the official US prints are 4.80 for 8 September and 4.83 for 9 September, both below the level quoted here.

Sentio became the second pollster to put the Liberals above the threshold, at 5.1 percent, after Novus's 5.8 on Wednesday; DN/Ipsos had 3.5 on 7 September. 2 · The IAEA board refers Iran to the UN Security Council

The 35-member board voted 23 to 3 with 8 abstentions on Wednesday in Vienna, on a British, French, German and American text asking the director general to report Iran's non-compliance. Russia, China and Niger opposed. It is the first referral in twenty years; the previous one was in February 2006.

Agence France-Presse via France 24 (center, T1); Associated Press via the Los Angeles Times (center, T1); Reuters via Al-Monitor (center, T1); Washington Post (left, T1); Washington Examiner (right, T2); Al Jazeera (center-left, T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The twenty-year claim survives only in this precise form. The board found Iran in non-compliance on 12 June 2025 and outlets used the same phrase then, for the finding rather than a referral; an unqualified version would silently claim both and be false. A desk line saying inspectors have had no access since June 2025 was narrowed: the agency says it has not regained access to the struck sites, which is not the same as no inspection at all.

3 · Sweden's early-vote registration fault narrows to 500 ballots

An information technology fault at the Election Authority, reported on 4 September, double-marked or misattributed early votes. An initial estimate of about 4,000 affected voters was revised to at most 500 whose votes cannot be identified. The authority says no voters were affected in at least 114 of the 290 municipalities.

Valmyndigheten, val.se press releases read directly (primary, T1); Aftonbladet, Sveriges Radio and Omni for the original estimate (center to left, T2); TV4, Svenska Dagbladet and Dagens Nyheter for the revision (center to center-left, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. A desk line saying 203 municipalities are cleared and 87 still checking was cut: those sum to exactly 290, the signature of a constructed pair, and the authority's own published figure is at least 114 unaffected. A claim of 2.7 million early votes, up 25 percent on 2022, was cut as unverifiable; the authority said on 8 September only that more than two million had voted early. The fault is dated to the day it was reported, because no start date is published.
