1 · The World

1 · The Bank of Japan raises to 1.25 percent, and the yen falls anyway

The Policy Board voted 7-2 to lift the uncollateralised overnight call rate to around 1.25 percent from 24 September, a 31-year high. Asada Toichiro and Sato Ayano dissented. The yen weakened past 157 per dollar rather than strengthening; the ten-year Japanese government bond yield sat about 2.99 percent.

Bank of Japan, “Change in the Guideline for Money Market Operations”, the statement PDF fetched and its text extracted directly (primary, T1); Nikkei Asia, Bloomberg and the Associated Press (center and wire, T1); Al Jazeera (leans left, T1); CNBC (center, T2). Verification: verified, primary source. The Bank's own words are “decided, by a 7-2 majority vote”, “remain at around 1.25 percent” and “effective from September 24, 2026”, with the voting note naming both dissenters. The 31-year high is carried independently by four outlets. The 2.99 percent is a vendor reading and the yield actually eased about a hundredth on the day, which is why this gives a level and not a direction. Worth knowing for the next one: this paper had been told the Bank's statement PDF was unreadable, and it is not; it extracts in full, which is how the dissenters' names got here.

2 · Trump says he is weighing whether to annihilate Iran's government

Trump told Axios: “Do I want to go in and annihilate them or do I not?” Iran's Revolutionary Guard struck a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz early Friday, the second vessel hit that day, saying the passage was illegal. He meets the leaders of six Gulf states on Tuesday, the same states whose objection stopped this in August.

Axios interview (center, T2); CBS News live updates citing UK Maritime Trade Operations (leans left, T1); Al Arabiya English and the Jerusalem Post (lean right, T2); Anadolu (center, T2). Verification: the tanker strike is verified, reliable secondary; the quotation is single source, unconfirmed, one Axios interview carried widely but not independently obtained. Two precisions. Trump's word was “them”: “the Iranian regime” is Axios's own bracketed gloss, which is what the headline rests on. And a repair: the draft gave the strike a time of 03:47, which is when a live blog posted, not when the strike happened, and no source states the incident time at all. The strait remains restricted, not closed.

3 · Russia and China veto United Nations monitoring of Iran sanctions

The Security Council failed 11 to 2, with Pakistan and Somalia abstaining, as Russia and China vetoed the American resolution renewing the expert panel that monitors the sanctions reimposed on Iran by the 2025 snapback. The panel's mandate expires 27 September.

The Associated Press via Local 10 (center wire, T1, 15:56); Al Jazeera (leans left, T2, 21:31 GMT); the Washington Times carrying the Associated Press (leans right, T2); the Security Council's own record and press.un.org SC/16455 for the mandate expiry (primary, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The consequence, in plain terms: the sanctions stay legally in force, but after 27 September no independent body reports who is breaking them, so enforcement becomes a matter of national claims rather than United Nations findings. The 27 September date appears nowhere in the wire coverage and came from the Council's own record, which is where any Council item with a deadline in it should be checked.

SVT Nyheter live blog (center public broadcaster, T1); Reuters, Simon Johnson and Terje Solsvik (center wire, T1); TV4 and Nyheter24 (center, T2); Dagens Opinion's published running order of the Speaker's meetings (center, T2); Altinget (center, T2). Reuters via U.S. News and via Insurance Journal (center wire, T1); the National (center, T2); Al Jazeera (leans left, T1); the Washington Examiner (leans right, T2). President Lee Jae Myung said repeatedly that Seoul will not deploy troops or military assets amounting to involvement in a conflict, resisting American pressure. He left open expanded naval protection of Korean commercial vessels. The Middle East share of Korean crude imports has fallen from about 70 percent early this year to roughly half.

The New York Times and NBC News (lean left, T1); the Korea Times and the Korea Herald (center, T2); the Korea JoongAng Daily (leans right, T2); United Press International (center wire, T2); the Korea Economic Institute and Asia Business Daily on the import shares. Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Two repairs, and the second is the interesting one. “Said three times” was not supported by any account reached. More importantly, the 70 percent Hormuz dependence figure the draft gave as current was accurate in March 2026 and is now roughly half, with non-Middle East crude at 51.5 percent of imports over May to July. A true earlier fact presented as a current one is an error, and national energy dependence moves fast in a shipping war.

3 · Energy

1 · Crude heads for its first weekly loss in a month while diesel sets a record

Brent fell a third session to about 103 dollars, heading for its first weekly loss after three weeks of gains, as Aramco marketed roughly 60 million barrels of ship-to-ship cargoes off Oman's Sohar across September and October loadings. The East-West pipeline stayed shut an eighth day. The American Automobile Association's national diesel average hit a record 6.45 dollars a gallon.

CNBC (center, T1); OilPrice.com and Cornerstone Futures (center, T2); Anadolu (center, T2, 14:08); Bloomberg (center wire, T1, 16 Sep); Energy Intelligence (trade, T2); gasprices.aaa.com fetched directly with its own “price as of” date (primary, T1); the Energy Information Administration's weekly on-highway average of 6.285 for the week of 14 September (primary, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary, with the retail diesel figure verified, primary source. Four repairs, and the largest was a number that matched nothing: the draft carried retail diesel at 6.26 dollars, which is neither the Automobile Association's same-day 6.45, nor its 14 September 6.23, nor the Energy Information Administration's weekly 6.285. Three agencies publish the same price on three clocks, so the copy now names the agency and the day. The weekly loss was also written as settled when every source has it prospective and the week had not closed; the Brent decimal was softened because session readings ran from 102.57 to 104.69; and “WTI briefly below 100” was Thursday's move, not Friday's. The shape of the story: the market has decided Saudi barrels reach buyers through the strait even with the pipeline shut, so crude is falling into an active shooting war while products set records.

Ministers opened a two-day informal council at Dublin Castle on an EU-wide levy pushed in a joint letter by Germany, Italy, Austria, Poland, Spain and Portugal. Valdis Dombrovskis declined to propose one, saying member states control their own taxes. Ireland's Simon Harris will ask the Commission to work up models for October's Luxembourg council.

Euronews (center, T2); RTÉ (center, T1); Reuters via Investing.com (center wire, T1); Eunews (center, T3). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. Two editor doubts were resolved against the sources rather than by preference: France is not among the six and is not named as a backer anywhere reachable, so it stays out, and the council runs 18 and 19 September rather than one day. A named German minister was dropped because the push is attributed to the six finance ministries collectively. The consequence worth seeing: a Commission that will not propose while six capitals push produces a patchwork of national levies, which fragments the single energy market rather than taxing it.

4 · Economy

1 · The Bank of Japan tightened into slowing measured inflation

Consumer prices excluding fresh food rose 1.7 percent in August, easing from 1.8 and below the 2 percent target, in a release that crossed hours before the decision. The Bank's statement cites the risk that underlying inflation deviates above 2 percent and says it will continue raising the policy rate.

The Statistics Bureau of Japan's August release, fetched directly: all items 102.2 at +1.9 percent year on year, excluding fresh food 102.0 at +1.7, excluding fresh food and energy 102.5 at +1.9 (primary, T1, 08:30 Japan time); the Bank of Japan statement PDF (primary, T1); the Japan Times and Reuters (center, T1). Verification: verified, primary source. Both claims are the Bank's own words: “there is a risk that it will deviate upward to a level above the price stability target of 2 percent” and “the Bank will continue to raise the policy interest rate”. Two things worth naming. This is a central bank tightening against expected energy pass-through rather than against measured prices, and its own statement twice blames “the situation in the Middle East” for the weakness in its growth picture. And Japan publishes three consumer price measures; almost every outlet today quotes 1.7 percent without saying which one it is.

2 · The oil retreat unwinds part of the Federal Reserve shock

The ten-year Treasury closed 4.94 percent on Thursday against 5.01 the day before, the thirty-year 5.29 against 5.35, and the S&P 500 rose 1.14 percent to 7,637.76, its best session since early August, as Brent fell.

The US Treasury daily par yield curve file, fetched directly (primary, T1, Thursday close); Federal Reserve Economic Data SP500, Thursday's 7,637.76 against Wednesday's 7,551.81 (primary, T1). Verification: verified, primary source. The percentage was recomputed at 1.1381, and “best session since early August” was tested rather than accepted: every daily change back to mid-July was computed, and the last larger session was 4 August at +1.79 percent, with nothing above +1.06 in between. The claim holds. What it means is worth stating plainly: the entire rates repricing of the past month has been an oil story, so a supply fix moves borrowing costs worldwide faster than any central bank statement did.

3 · Washington holds back a 7.5 percent China tariff until after the summit

The United States will withhold its Section 301 excess-capacity report, which recommends a 7.5 percent tariff on Chinese goods, until after the Trump-Xi summit on 24 September. Washington is seeking a six-month truce extension; the current truce expires 10 November.

Bloomberg, “US Said to Delay Excess Capacity Tariffs Until After Xi Summit” (center, T1); the Korea Times, Transport Topics and Bloomberg Law, all carrying it (T2). Verification: single source, unconfirmed. The circular-sourcing test came back positive and cheaply: one carrier names Bloomberg in its own text, and no outlet has independent sourcing. The delay, the 7.5 percent figure, the six-month ask and the 10 November expiry all rest on one Bloomberg exclusive. The Section 301 excess-capacity investigation itself is independently documented, opened in March against China, South Korea, Japan and thirteen other economies, so the probe is solid and only the delay is not. Poland's prime minister told parliament there is “a real risk that drones or other types of projectiles will enter the territory of one or several countries on the eastern flank and even cause destruction”, which Russia would present as an accident so that NATO sees no reason to respond. He cited NATO, Ukrainian and American intelligence, naming the Central Intelligence Agency.

The Associated Press via Local 10 (center wire, T1, 18:42); the Washington Post (leans left, T1); Bloomberg (center, T1). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The quotation matches the Associated Press text word for word, which is why it is attributed there: Euronews runs a materially different English translation of the same Polish, using “NATO countries” and “missiles”, and mixing one outlet's translation with another's attribution is how a quotation stops being a quotation. Why it matters to this reader: a sitting NATO prime minister naming a deliberate strategy of deniable attacks sets the terms on which every eastern-flank member, Sweden included, will read the next drone over its territory.

Trump said that if making Canada the bloc's first associate member is “at all a hostile act, I will put very serious tariffs or stop trading with Europe on many things”. Ursula von der Leyen proposed the associate membership on Wednesday; he called the prospect laughable.

The mechanics worth knowing: associate membership is not a category that exists in the European Union treaties, so what was floated is a new arrangement rather than an accession step, and it has now become a live trade-war trigger against Europe's largest export market.

Russia began three days of voting in its first State Duma election since the full-scale invasion, with 111 million eligible voters. Yabloko, the country's oldest liberal party and the only registered party running on a pro-peace platform, is excluded from the national party list. Polls close Sunday.

The Associated Press via ABC News, KSAT and Local 10 (center wire, T1); Meduza (center, T2); the Moscow Times, Al Jazeera, Euronews and RTÉ on the Yabloko exclusion, independently of each other (T2). Verification: verified, reliable secondary. The load-bearing detail was checked specifically rather than taken on trust: the Russian Supreme Court barred Yabloko from the party-list ballot in August, upheld on 17 August, and separately 32 of its members were barred from single-mandate races. “Excluded from the national list” is exact and is deliberately not loosened to “banned from the election”. The result lands on Sunday, and this paper does not run at weekends, so it is Monday's story.
